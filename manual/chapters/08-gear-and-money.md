# Gear & Money {#ch-8}

## 8-1. Paying for Things {#sec-8-1}

Every purchase can be made in two ways, and the choice matters.

- **Official channels — Credit Score.** Corporate shops never take your money; they extend you debt. The item's Credit Score cost is subtracted from your Credit Score, and the purchase is **logged against your identity**. Cheap for groceries and rent, ruinous for anything that matters on a job: the more attention an item draws, the steeper its cost.
- **The street — physical cash.** Untraceable, illegal, and expensive: the listed price **×3 to ×10**, depending on local risk (the GM sets the markup). No trace.

**Rarity** runs from **F** (everyday) to **A** (prototype). The two rarest tiers, **A and B**, never appear in official shops: street cash only.

> Credit is the leash the system uses to keep the lower classes compliant. A corporate sweep team simply gets issued its equipment; you will have to find yours.

::table[economy]

### Your stash {#sec-8-stash}

Not everything has to be on you. Gear tab → **Stashes** (right column, each stash folded to one line until you tap it): name the place (a locker at the bus depot, a loose tile under the sink) and 📦 on an item puts one of it there; cash goes in and out by the amount. What is stashed is not carried: it can't be lost, searched, taken or used until you go back for it — which, at the table, means the GM agrees you can get there. Out of reach during a fight. Only you and the GM see it; every move is logged.

**Crew stashes.** At the table the GM can set up stashes the whole crew shares — the van, a lock-up, a dead drop. They show on everyone's Gear tab: anyone can put things in (📦 asks which stash) or take them out, and the stash's history shows who did what. Trust is the point. The GM can **lock** one (you see it, you can't reach it: it's being watched, or the van is impounded) or **raid** it (everything in it is gone).

### The washer {#sec-8-washer}

Some fixers move money between the two worlds. **Washing** turns cash into Credit Score: a fake salary, a fake contract, and your score goes up. **Cashing out** goes the other way: you borrow against your score and walk out with untraced cash, and your score goes down. The fixer names a rate (cash per Credit Score point) and takes a commission; the GM makes the offer, it appears in Gear, and you choose how many points to take — the sheet shows the sum before you agree. Credit Score never goes above its cap or below 0 this way. Every deal is logged: a fixer's books are a trail of their own.

## 8-2. Weapons {#sec-8-2}

Every weapon attacks with a skill: knives, batons and stun guns use **Melee / CQC**, pistols **Handguns**, SMGs and assault rifles **Automatic Firearms**, sniper rifles **Long Guns / Shotguns**, fists **Brawling**. Damage adds your Offensive Bonus.

::table[weapons]

### Weapon mods {#sec-8-2-mods}

Weapons take small upgrades in **mod slots** by rarity: F and E 1, D and C 2, B 3, A 4 (fists and the stun gun none). **One mod of each kind per weapon.** Melee: a *Balanced Hilt* (+1 TN) and a *Sharpened Edge* (+1 damage). Firearms: a *Custom Grip* (+1 TN), a *Heavy Barrel* (+1 damage), *Improved Sights* (+25% maximum range) and an *Extended Magazine* (+50%, at least one more round). Anyone can buy them; **a Soldier or a Tech Wizard installs and removes them**, out of a fight, for free — *🔧* on the weapon's Gear row. A modded weapon becomes its own item (marked *Modded*): its mods go with it when you sell, trade, stash or give it, and it still loads its stock magazines. Taking off the last mod makes it a stock weapon again. Prices are provisional.

::table[weapon-mods]

## 8-3. Armor {#sec-8-3}

Each piece adds its **armor value** to your armor: your armor is the sum of what you wear (plus cover in a fight). Visibility tells how obvious the armor is to a casual observer (1 = discreet, 5 = unmistakable).

Armor **cracks** by 1 when it stops a location attack completely, or when an effect strips it. Cracks stay until the armor is **repaired** — during downtime, or with a Tech Wizard's *Field Maintenance* — or until you change what you wear. Normal hits never crack armor (see 7-5).

::table[armor]

**ID Scramble Kits** are not armor: a balaclava, gloves and a shell layer for heists and entries, worn in the full-body slot with no protection, sold for street cash only. Putting one on gives its condition, taking it off ends it. The basic kit makes you **Masked** (no visual ID, no prints or DNA — but obviously masked, so treated as hostile on sight); the enhanced kit makes you **Unreadable** (visual, thermal and gait scans fail too — but automated security flags you on sight: a monitoring alert). The GM sees the condition on your card.

## 8-4. Items & Tools {#sec-8-4}

**Using items.** Consumables — kits, stims, antidotes, grenades — have a **Use** button in the inventory, and *Use item* on the Turn card (**1 action** in combat, free outside). Pick the item and, if it offers a choice, what it does; medical items work **on you** or **on an ally next to you** (the window shows the result, e.g. "heal 7", and they enter it on their own sheet). Heals are rolled by the app or typed from your dice. **Timed effects** (+2 DEX for 3 rounds…) show as a green pill on the status bar, count down at each round end and raise every number built on them; strong stims end in a **crash** (Slowed for a round). Items sold in packs ("×5") give that many. Smoke, EMP, darts and the like change the battlefield: the sheet spends the action and logs the effect, the GM applies it.

**Tools.** Gear you carry — lockpicks, climbing hooks, AR glasses, a crowbar — helps a roll when it fits: the roll window lists the tools for that skill, attribute or attack as tick boxes ("Mechanical Lockpick Set +2 · physical locks"). Tick the one that applies; **one tool counts per roll**, nothing is used up, and the GM can say it doesn't apply. Always-on gear (AR glasses for Perception, a laser sight for gun attacks) starts ticked. The log names the tool. A **gas mask** in your inventory makes you immune to gas.

::table[items]

## 8-5. Hacker Decks & Mods {#sec-8-5}

A deck sets the Hacker's **RAM** and **heat cap**. Mod slots take hardware upgrades: **RAM expansions** add RAM, **cooling upgrades** raise the heat cap. Battery life has no rule yet — use it for narrative pressure.

::table[decks]

::table[deck-mods]

### Tech Wizard drones {#sec-8-5-drones}

The Tech Wizard's drone is an item, bought and modded like a deck — and every Tech Wizard starts with a free **Scrap Drone**, their personal project. It never takes a turn: the Tech Wizard spends actions on the abilities that use it. Its numbers go into them: the Engineer's *Deploy Sentry* barrier is the drone hovering, the sentry turret is the drone landing (its HP, armor and damage). And it gives **reach**: *Healing Surge*, *Field Maintenance* and *Rig Power Conduit*, normally next to you, work up to the drone's reach away. Mod slots take a longer link (reach), a frame (HP), plating (armor), a payload (turret damage) or a shield emitter (armor for allies behind the barrier). When it's shot down, mark it **Wrecked** on the engine: it never shuts down, but it works at half reach and half damage until *Restock & Repair* or the time between missions fixes it. All values are provisional.

::table[drones]

::table[drone-mods]

### Scavenger optics {#sec-8-5-optics}

An optic sets the Scavenger's **Range**, **Track** and **mode** (5-6). Anyone can buy one; only a Scavenger uses the numbers.

::table[optics]

## Ammo, misc items and trading {#sec-8-ammo}

**Magazines.** Every gun has its own spare magazine in the market's *Ammo* tab (your guns' magazines come first, marked *fits your gun*). A magazine costs 10% of the gun's price (at least $5), and **every gun comes with 2 spares**. *Reload* loads a spare and discards the one in the gun; with no spares left you can't reload — buy some, or ask the GM. The weapon card shows how many spares you have.

**Bursts.** SMGs and assault rifles fire **3-round bursts**: each attack uses 3 rounds, so a 30-round magazine is 10 attacks. The sheet counts it for you ("10 attacks left"). Abilities use the ammunition they say.

**Custom items.** The market's *Misc* tab adds anything worth tracking — a keycard, a sealed package, a photo — to a *Misc* group: no price, no rules. If it cost money, change your cash by hand.

**Unique items.** Sometimes you find something one of a kind — an experimental smart pistol, a heavily modded deck. The GM forges it and sends it to whoever found it; it shows in **purple** with a *unique* tag, with a one-line note ("◆ Smart-link: locks onto body heat") on the weapon card. Its bonuses apply while it's in use: a weapon in a slot, armor worn, the active deck or optic, an item carried. It keeps every stat if you hand it to someone, it's never on sale, and some can't be fenced at all. Its magazines are in the market's Ammo tab like any other gun's.

**Trash and trade.** Every inventory row has 🗑 (drop one, after asking) and ⇄ (trade): **sell** it for half the list price — a quarter if you bought it officially (registered gear is built to age fast; street-bought units are sold first) — (custom items: type what you got), or **give** it to a player at the table — it lands on their sheet even if their tablet is asleep, and in combat it costs 1 action (Interact). Everything is logged, so the GM sees where things went.

## 8-6. Lifestyle {#sec-8-6}

The monthly cost of existing. Operators start at the bottom; moving up is something you earn. Pick yours in the market's Lifestyle tab with **Choose**: it charges nothing. Some categories hold **one** choice and a new one replaces it — housing, data plan, food: a new flat replaces the old one. Others **add up** — bills, transport, fees, a club — except where two entries are alternatives of each other (basic or premium medical, plain or ad-free streams). Each market row says which (*adds to the others* / *replaces the one you have*); ✕ removes one. The sheet shows the monthly upkeep in $ (or ≈ Credit Score if paid officially); the GM's downtime report shows it for the time that passed, and the table settles it however it likes. It has no other mechanical effect yet.

::table[lifestyle]

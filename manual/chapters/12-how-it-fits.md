# How It Fits Together {#ch-12}

The rules chapters say *what* each rule does. This chapter says *why* it is there, *who* decides it and *what it leans on*. One page per system, always in the same order:

- **What it's for** — the piece of the world the rule stands for.
- **The rule in one line** — the whole thing, short enough to remember.
- **Who decides** — the app, the GM or the player; and what the app never does by itself.
- **How it connects** — what feeds it, and what it feeds.
- **Say it at the table** — a few sentences to read aloud when a player asks.
- **Where to look** — the full rule, and the GM's side.

> **The golden thread.** The app counts, remembers and reminds. The GM decides what the world does. The players decide what their characters do. When a rule seems to "happen by itself", it is because nobody had a choice to make there — a die, a sum, a timer.

## 12-1. The shape of a scene {#sec-12-1}

Most of the game happens in one loop. Each stage has its own pressures; the systems below bite at the stages listed here.

| Stage | What happens | What bites |
|---|---|---|
| **Explore** | The crew moves freely on the map; talk, search, hack, sneak. | Checks and difficulty · light · **site alert** and **sensors** · networks and doors · hazards · **Strain** (the Hum, a fumble, debt at a mission's start) |
| **Chase** | Somebody runs: legs of one move each, the gap from 0 to 6 — off the map until it stops. | Group checks · help · cover fire and hacks · complications through ⚡ Harm · Strain and CON from leg 5 |
| **Contact** | Someone spots an enemy waiting on the map: everyone stops. | Fog of war · intel · the GM's choice: talk, sneak past, or fight |
| **Prepare** | The get-ready window: initiative, loadout, a last move. A *Head start* gives a round 0 to an ambush. | Initiative · surprise · prep actions |
| **Fight** | Rounds and actions, hits and cover. | Sight and cover · crits and fumbles · conditions · overwatch · **Strain** (half HP, a crewmate down) · crew help |
| **After** | Bodies, loot, breath. | Healing · a safe *New scene* (Strain −1) · intel · the site stays as alert as you left it |
| **Between missions** | Time passes. | Downtime · debt · experience and levels · crew and Unity |

## 12-2. Checks and difficulty {#sec-12-2}

**What it's for.** Most of what Operators do is uncertain without being dramatic: picking a lock, reading a room, talking past a clerk. A check settles it in one roll and moves the story on — success or failure, something happens.

**The rule in one line.** Roll a d20 equal to or under your TN (a skill, an attribute or your Class TN), minus the difficulty the GM sets for the situation.

**Who decides.**
- **The player** picks *what* they try and *how* — the skill follows from the means.
- **The GM** sets the difficulty — one tier for the whole situation, from *Very easy* (+6) to *Nightmare* (−10) — and says what failure costs. The GM can lock the tier on every sheet.
- **The app** works out the TN (attributes, skills, talents, items), suggests the light row, adds the tools you carry that fit, shows the site alert and Strain when they bite, and rolls — or takes the number from your real die. It never decides what a success means.

**How it connects.**
- *Fed by:* attributes and skills (character), tools and boosts (gear), talents (experience), light (12-3), the site alert (12-10), Strain (12-12), conditions (12-6).
- *Feeds:* everything — doors, hacking, intel, puzzles. A natural 20 adds Strain.
- *Also:* **contested checks** (both roll; the better margin wins), **passive scores** (TN − 5, the GM checks quietly) **help** — one crewmate who says how rolls a fitting skill: +2 on your next check, −2 on a natural 20, one helper at a time — and **group checks** (everyone rolls, half must succeed).

**Say it at the table.**
> "Tell me what you do and how. You roll a d20 and want to get your number or lower. I'll tell you how hard it is — the app shows it all before you roll. Either way, something happens. If someone can help, they roll first: a good roll gives you +2."

**Where to look.** [5-3, skills](#/manual/sec-5-3), [6-2, difficulty](#/manual/sec-6-2), [helping](#/manual/sec-6-2-help), [group checks](#/manual/sec-6-2-group). The GM's side: [9-4](#/manual/sec-9-4).

## 12-3. Light and darkness {#sec-12-3}

**What it's for.** Half the city runs on rationed power. A dark corridor is a place to hide — and a place where you see nothing. Light is a tactical resource the crew can make, break and carry.

**The rule in one line.** In the dark, Stealth gets easier (+2), Perception and shooting get harder (−2 to −4) — unless you bring night vision, thermal or a light.

**Who decides.**
- **The app** knows where it's dark: the map's environment (bright or dark), darkness tiles, lights that are on (and powered), torches and flares. It suggests the right row in the check window.
- **The player** confirms the row — the suggestion can be wrong, you can change it.
- **The GM** switches the environment and the lights, and cuts the power.

**How it connects.**
- *Fed by:* the map (lights, power, breakers), gear (torches, flares, night vision, thermal).
- *Feeds:* Stealth, Perception and attacks · fog of war (what the crew sees) · cameras (a camera sees nothing in the dark).
- *Pushed by:* cutting a breaker kills the lights on its wires — and the cameras' eyes.

**Say it at the table.**
> "If it's dark where you stand, sneaking is easier and seeing is harder. Your sheet will suggest the light for each roll — change it if it's wrong. A torch helps you see, and helps everyone see you."

**Where to look.** [7-2, line of sight](#/manual/sec-7-2). The GM's side: [9-4](#/manual/sec-9-4), [9-8](#/manual/sec-9-8).

## 12-4. Sight, cover and range {#sec-12-4}

**What it's for.** Fights in 2045 are short and ugly, and they're won by position. Where you stand matters more than how hard you hit.

**The rule in one line.** Cover is armor: partial +2, full +4 on every hit you take; walls block sight; your weapon has a range.

**Who decides.**
- **The app** traces the line from square centre to square centre and tells anyone who taps a marker: clear, partial cover or blocked, and whether it's in range. It's information.
- **The GM** confirms the cover a target gets (the map suggests it) and calls flanking — a flanked target loses its cover.
- **The player** chooses where to stand. Cover never cracks; armor can.

**How it connects.**
- *Fed by:* the map (walls, doors, props that give cover, scrap cover placed in a fight).
- *Feeds:* every attack's damage · overwatch (only what you can see) · fog of war · cameras (walls block them too).
- *Undone by:* a critical hit (ignores armor and cover), flanking, a breached wall.

**Say it at the table.**
> "Behind something solid you're much harder to hurt — half-cover takes 2 off every hit, full cover 4. Tap anyone on the map to see if you have a line on them and if they're in range."

**Where to look.** [7-2](#/manual/sec-7-2), [7-5](#/manual/sec-7-5). The GM's side: [9-6](#/manual/sec-9-6), [9-8](#/manual/sec-9-8).

## 12-5. Critical hits and fumbles {#sec-12-5}

**What it's for.** A well-placed shot ends a fight; a jammed gun at the wrong moment ends you. Crits and fumbles are the moments everyone at the table remembers.

**The rule in one line.** A natural 1, or a hit by 10 or more (8 with keen weapons), is a critical: it ignores armor and cover. A natural 20, or a miss by 10 or more, is a fumble: guns may jam, melee leaves you Exposed, thrown weapons are lost.

**Who decides.**
- **The app** shows the odds before you roll (your crit and fumble ranges), works out the result, runs the jam check (a d10 against the gun's reliability) and marks your hit CRITICAL for the GM.
- **The GM** applies an enemy's crit or fumble — enemies only crit and fumble on the natural 1 and 20 — and narrates both.
- **The player** clears a jam with Reload.

**How it connects.**
- *Fed by:* weapons (keen weapons, reliability), mods (*Keen Edge*, *Match Trigger*), your TN (a higher TN reaches the margin more often).
- *Feeds:* damage (armor and cover ignored) · conditions (Exposed) · ammo (a jammed gun) · **Strain** (a fumble or a natural 20 adds +1).

**Say it at the table.**
> "Roll a 1, or beat your number by 10, and it's a critical — straight through armor and cover. Roll a 20, or miss by 10, and something goes wrong: your gun might jam, or you leave yourself open. The attack window shows your chances before you roll."

**Where to look.** [7-5, critical hits](#/manual/sec-7-5-crit). The GM's side: [9-6](#/manual/sec-9-6).

## 12-6. Conditions and injuries {#sec-12-6}

**What it's for.** A wound isn't just a number. A broken arm drops your gun; a crippled leg keeps you in place. Conditions turn damage into situations to solve.

**The rule in one line.** A condition changes what you can do for a while — some for a turn, some until the end of the fight, injuries until someone treats them.

**Who decides.**
- **The app** adds conditions from location hits, abilities and hazards, applies their numbers on your sheet (you see both values) and clears the short ones when they wear off.
- **The GM** puts conditions on enemies, and on players when the story says so.
- **The player** treats injuries — First aid, a Trauma Patch, or downtime long enough.

**How it connects.**
- *Fed by:* location attacks (arm, leg, abdomen, chest, head), fumbles (Exposed), abilities (Focused, Suppressed, Marked…), hazards, HP 0 (Incapacitated).
- *Feeds:* TN and damage · movement · actions · what the enemies see on your card (visible conditions only).

**Say it at the table.**
> "Some hits leave a mark: a broken arm, a crippled leg, knocked prone. The sheet shows what each one does and takes it off when it wears off. Injuries stay until somebody patches you up."

**Where to look.** [7-6, conditions](#/manual/sec-7-6), [7-5, location attacks](#/manual/sec-7-5-location).

## 12-7. Contact, Prepare and the Head start {#sec-12-7}

**What it's for.** Fights don't start with a bell. Somebody turns a corner. The moment between "they're there" and "it's on" is where plans happen — or fall apart.

**The rule in one line.** When a player's marker sees an enemy waiting, everyone stops (*Contact!*); the GM then opens *Prepare* (initiative and a last move) or lets the crew go on; an ambush gets a *Head start* — a round 0 where only the crew acts.

**Who decides.**
- **The app** notices contact (what the players' markers can see, light and walls included), stops the map and tells everyone; it runs the get-ready window and the initiative.
- **The GM** decides what contact means — a fight, a conversation, a chance to slip past — and how many prep actions a Head start gives; *complete surprise* means the enemies can't react in round 1.
- **The players** spend prep actions in any order: move, take cover, ping, ready abilities.

**How it connects.**
- *Fed by:* fog of war and light (who sees whom) · the map being shown (a hidden map spots nobody) · intel (knowing what you're facing).
- *Feeds:* the fight (initiative, surprise) · Strain later (a crewmate going down).
- *Beside it:* the **site alert** (12-10) is the site noticing the crew; contact is the crew noticing the enemy.

**Say it at the table.**
> "When you spot someone waiting, everything stops — you get a breath to decide. If it turns into a fight, you'll get a get-ready window; if you set up an ambush, you might get a free round before they move."

**Where to look.** [6-4, surprise](#/manual/sec-6-4), [7-1, initiative](#/manual/sec-7-1). The GM's side: [9-6](#/manual/sec-9-6), [9-8](#/manual/sec-9-8).

## 12-8. Overwatch {#sec-12-8}

**What it's for.** Holding a corridor. Covering a friend who runs. Making the enemy think twice before crossing a line.

**The rule in one line.** Spend an action to watch a quarter circle in one of 8 directions, out to your weapon's range; the first enemy that moves into it can be shot once — or let past — until your next turn.

**Who decides.**
- **The player** picks the direction, then takes or passes the shot when it's offered.
- **The app** works out the squares you can actually see in that quarter, and checks every enemy the GM moves against it.
- **The GM** confirms each shot — nothing fires by itself.

**How it connects.**
- *Fed by:* sight and range (12-4), your weapon in hand.
- *Feeds:* a free attack (with its own crits and fumbles) · enemy movement (the GM may route around it).

**Say it at the table.**
> "Pick a direction and hold it. The first one who steps into that area, I'll ask you: shoot, or let them pass? You keep watching until your next turn."

**Where to look.** [7-3, actions](#/manual/sec-7-3). The GM's side: [9-6](#/manual/sec-9-6).

## 12-9. Networks, doors and hacking {#sec-12-9}

**What it's for.** Every building runs on networks: cameras, keypads, doors, the lights. A hacker sees that layer; everyone else sees walls. Doors are where the two meet.

**The rule in one line.** A ping lists what hangs on the networks within 10 squares — never where; doors come in three kinds: a handle and a lock (Sleight of Hand), electric (Electronics & Security at the door), networked (from the scan, remotely — the GM works it).

**Who decides.**
- **The app** answers a ping from the full map (types and counts, named pieces by name), remembers locked doors you tried, and tells the GM what you're asking for.
- **The GM** calls the roll and works the door; decides what a hack does to the world (a camera looped, a network cut).
- **The player** chooses how to get through: pick it, short it, hack it — or go round.

**How it connects.**
- *Fed by:* the map's networks (uplinks, routers, access points, wires and Wi-Fi), power (no power, no network — some doors fail locked).
- *Feeds:* the **site alert** (every ping is heard; forcing networked doors may add Trace) · **sensors** (a cut or looped network silences its cameras) · the hacker's engine (RAM, heat).

**Say it at the table.**
> "A ping tells you what's on the networks around you — cameras, keypads, doors — but not where. Some doors you pick, some you short at the panel, some you can work from your deck once you've found them. Every ping is heard."

**Where to look.** [7-4, the Hacker](#/manual/sec-7-4-hacker). The GM's side: [9-7](#/manual/sec-9-7), [9-8](#/manual/sec-9-8).

## 12-10. The site alert and its sensors {#sec-12-10}

**What it's for.** In 2045 security is not a guard at a door: it is a network. Patrols wear AR feeds, cameras and sensors stream to a local AI, and somebody reads what the AI flags. A site doesn't notice *you*; it notices that something is off — a camera gone dark, a guard who stopped checking in, a door that opened on its own.

**The rule in one line.** Every site has one alert level — *Calm, Twitchy, Alert, Lockdown* — and on Alert quiet, technical work is −1 TN on that map (−2 on Lockdown).

**Who decides.**
- **The GM** turns the level, by the story: what the crew did, what the site would make of it. A reminder of how the site behaves at each level sits beside the dial (Twitchy: a technician reads the logs; Alert: nobody wanders, patrols go in pairs; Lockdown: sealed, keys changed, the response on its way).
- **The app** adds what the networks hear by themselves: a hacker's ping (1 on a success, more on a failure, more on a well-guarded network) and a toy drone (1). It also notices when a player walks into a working sensor's view, or across a laser that is on, and gives the GM a line — with *+1 Trace* or *Ask Stealth* as one-tap answers.
- **The app never** raises the level for a sensor, locks a door or arms anything by itself; the GM taps it. The level never drops by itself either.
- **The players** never see the dial. They see the site change — and the −1 in their check window once it bites.

**How it connects.**
- *Fed by:* pings and drones (hacking) · sensors and lasers (the map) · forced or hacked doors (networked doors) · the GM's reading of the story.
- *Feeds:* the TN of **Stealth, Sleight of Hand, Electronics & Security, Computers & Hacking** and the Hacker's Class TN rolls · the one-tap consequences: *Arm the sensors*, *Lock the networked doors* · the GM's narration (patrols, technicians, check-ins).
- *Pushed back by:* a hacker who loops or cuts a camera before the crew walks past (a sensor off its network says nothing) · *Clean Logs* and *Backdoor* (less Trace) · darkness (a camera sees nothing in the dark) · doing the job quietly.

**Say it at the table.**
> "The place is wired. Cameras, sensors, guards on comms — all of it feeds one system that's watching for anything odd. You won't see a meter; you'll see the building get nervous. Once it's on alert, sneaking, lockpicking and hacking all get harder until you leave. Kill the cameras first and they can't tell anyone."

**Where to look.** The rule: [6-4, The site's alert](#/manual/sec-6-4-alert). Pings: [7-4, the Hacker](#/manual/sec-7-4-hacker). The GM's side: [9-8, The map on the table](#/manual/sec-9-8) — *Site alert* and *Sensors that notice*.

## 12-11. Hazards {#sec-12-11}

**What it's for.** The places Operators work in are broken: live wires, gas leaks, steam lines, cold rooms. The environment is an enemy that doesn't take turns.

**The rule in one line.** A hazard zone hurts whoever is in it (Electric, Gas, Poison, Steam, Fire, Heat, Cold — each with its dice and the skill that deals with it); a valve or a breaker can switch it off.

**Who decides.**
- **The GM** draws the zones, switches them, shows or hides them, and sends the damage — rolled or typed, like any other die.
- **The app** notices who steps in, walks through or starts a turn in an active zone and gives the GM a line with the hit ready to send; players see a zone only when it's on and shown.
- **The player** gets out, takes the hit, or finds the control — working a valve or breaker is a request to the GM.

**How it connects.**
- *Fed by:* the map (zones, controls wired to them), power (breakers).
- *Feeds:* HP · conditions · **Strain** (below half HP) · the **Hum** (12-12), a zone that hurts the mind instead.
- **Falling** works the same way without a zone: the GM sends a fall (1d6 per full 3 m above 3 m); you land it with Agility & Athletics for half, or end up Prone; armor applies.
- **Anything else that hurts** (glass, a jolt, burning…): the GM's **⚡ Harm** — damage with armor or without, and your roll to avoid or halve it (no action), a condition if you fail.
- **Air and water:** *High water* is difficult ground; *Submerged* also starts a breath hold — CON rounds, then CON rolls at −1, −2…, then you black out and drown (1d6 a round). Smoke and gas: hold your breath by hand.

**Say it at the table.**
> "Some areas are dangerous to stand in — I'll tell you when you can see it. If you step in, you take the hit; somewhere there's usually a valve or a breaker that shuts it off."

**Where to look.** Falls, air and water: [6-8](#/manual/sec-6-8). The GM's side: [9-7, Building maps](#/manual/sec-9-7) — *Hazards*, and [9-8](#/manual/sec-9-8).

## 12-12. Strain and the Hum {#sec-12-12}

**What it's for.** HP measures what the world does to your body; Strain measures what it does to your head — the debt, the violence, the long nights, and the things in the signals that nobody can explain. It is not madness or a morality meter: it's wear. Characters who carry it start to fray, and one day they break.

**The rule in one line.** Strain runs 0 to 10; at 4 your skill checks are −1 TN, at 7 −2; at 10 you hit a breaking point (a d6 on the table), then drop back to 6.

**Who decides.**
- **The app** adds Strain when the rules say so: dropping below half HP (+1), a fumble or a natural 20 (+1), a crewmate going down (+2 to everyone else), debt at its worst when a mission starts (+1), a Hum zone (+1). It rolls nothing for you: every gain arrives as a card.
- **The player** answers the card: roll **Mental Strength** to steady yourself (a success halves the gain, rounded down — a single point vanishes) or just take it. At a breaking point the player rolls the d6 (and a second d6 for a Scar on a 6), on the app or with real dice.
- **The GM** adds Strain for what the story does (+1 / +2 on a player's card, or to everyone), hears about it when someone is *Fraying* or breaks (a line in *Needs you*), and plays the breaking point's result with the player.
- **The crew** can take it away: a crewmate's *Steady* (−1, −2 when the crew is Tight), a safe *New scene* (−1), downtime (more the longer it lasts, and the better your home).

**How it connects.**
- *Fed by:* HP (half), crits and fumbles, the crew (someone down), debt (the mission's first scene), hazards (the Hum), the GM.
- *Feeds:* every skill check's TN (not attacks) · breaking points and Scars · the GM's whispers (the frayed notice things the others don't).
- *Eased by:* Unity (a Tight crew steadies better; a broken one can't in a fight) · downtime and housing · safe scenes.
- **The Hum** is the uncanny edge of it: a hazard zone the GM draws, hidden by default, with no damage dice. Whoever steps in, walks through or starts a turn there gets +1 Strain — once per zone, until the GM switches it. Nobody else hears it.

**Say it at the table.**
> "Strain is your nerves. Bad things add to it — getting badly hurt, watching a friend drop, the debt calling. Every time it goes up you can roll Mental Strength to shrug off half. Past the middle it makes your skills shakier; at the top you break, and we roll to see how. Rest, a quiet moment and your crew bring it down."

**Where to look.** The rule: [6-7, Strain](#/manual/sec-6-7). The GM's side: [9-3, The sheet watch](#/manual/sec-9-3) — *Strain on the cards*; the Hum: [9-7, Building maps](#/manual/sec-9-7) — *Hazards*.

## 12-13. Intel {#sec-12-13}

**What it's for.** In a world of private armies and black-box drones, knowing what you're up against is half the fight. Intel is what your character has learned — on the job, from their past, from the crew.

**The rule in one line.** An enemy type you *Know* shows its armor, HP, movement and weapons; one you have *Studied* shows everything, and you get +1 to hit it.

**Who decides.**
- **The player** studies an enemy on the board (an action and a roll, once per fight), takes their background picks once, and shares an entry with the crew.
- **The app** keeps what each character knows and builds the facts from the GM's library — never from what a sheet sends.
- **The GM** grants intel when the story gives it (a briefing, a file, a survivor talking).

**How it connects.**
- *Fed by:* the life path (careers and events give background intel) · Study in a fight · the crew (Share) · the GM.
- *Feeds:* attacks (+1 against Studied types) · the crew's plan before a fight (12-7) · the Scavenger's reads.

**Say it at the table.**
> "If you know a type of enemy, your sheet shows what it's packing. Study one in a fight and you learn the rest — and hit it more easily from then on. Share what you know: the whole crew gets it."

**Where to look.** [7-9, Intel](#/manual/sec-7-9).

## 12-14. Crew and Unity {#sec-12-14}

**What it's for.** The crew is the only thing an Operator can count on — until it isn't. Unity measures whether they act together, never whether what they did was right.

**The rule in one line.** One shared track, 0 to 10: committed intent +1, overriding a crew that objected −1; a Tight crew (7+) gets a favour per mission, a Fractured one (3 or less) can't help each other in a fight.

**Who decides.**
- **The GM** opens a *Unity Check* when someone pushes the crew somewhere (who, and what they intend); records the result.
- **The players** vote in secret; only the tally is shown.
- **The app** keeps the track and shows its band on every sheet; it never judges the act.

**How it connects.**
- *Fed by:* Unity Checks · the story.
- *Feeds:* crew favours · help in a fight · **Strain** (a Tight crew steadies better; a broken one can't in a fight) · downtime events · mission hooks (a crew that splits).
- *Beside it:* each character's **Drive** (Justice or Ambition, plus a line) — never scored, but acting on it at a cost can earn Spotlight XP.

**Say it at the table.**
> "When one of you pushes the crew into something, we vote in secret. If the crew backs you, it holds together better; if you go ahead against them, it frays. It's not about right and wrong — it's about whether you're still a crew."

**Where to look.** [6-6, The Unity Protocol](#/manual/sec-6-6-unity), [Drives](#/manual/sec-6-6-drives). The GM's side: [9-10](#/manual/sec-9-10).

## 12-15. Debt, money and downtime {#sec-12-15}

**What it's for.** Nobody takes these jobs for fun. Every Operator owes someone, and the debt is the engine of the campaign: it's why you say yes.

**The rule in one line.** Credit Score is your debt allowance (official purchases spend it, traced); cash is street money (×3 to ×10, untraced); between missions downtime heals you, debt pressure rises by 1, and an instalment brings it down.

**Who decides.**
- **The GM** sends downtime (how long, an event for each player and the crew) and sets debt pressure when the story pushes.
- **The app** restores HP and armor (injuries too, if long enough), lowers Strain by the rest you get, shows upkeep and event options — never applies them — and tracks debt pressure.
- **The players** pay their instalments, buy, sell, stash, and settle the events at the table.

**How it connects.**
- *Fed by:* missions (pay — the GM's **🎁 Give**: a window you take, items, cash, Credit Score), what you find (containers on the map: a stash for the crew, a hidden part on a roll), the market, the washer (cash ↔ Credit Score), lifestyle (where you live).
- *Feeds:* **Strain** (debt at its worst adds +1 when a mission starts; downtime and a better home take it away) · downtime events · mission hooks.

**Say it at the table.**
> "Your Credit Score isn't money — it's how much the system lets you owe. Cash buys anything, quietly, at street prices. Between jobs you heal and the debt creeps up; pay an instalment to keep it off your back."

**Where to look.** [6-5, economy and debt](#/manual/sec-6-5), [5-10, downtime](#/manual/sec-5-10), [8-1, the market](#/manual/sec-8-1). The GM's side: [9-12](#/manual/sec-9-12).

## 12-16. Experience and levels {#sec-12-16}

**What it's for.** Operators get better by surviving. Experience rewards the job done, the danger faced and the moments that belonged to one character.

**The rule in one line.** XP comes from mission objectives, dangerous fights and the GM's Spotlight; 10 XP is a level, spent only when the GM opens level-up: a talent point every level, +1 attribute on even levels, +1 ability slot at 3, 6 and 9.

**Who decides.**
- **The GM** awards a mission's XP once (objectives, fights recorded as Hard or Deadly, Spotlight), opens level-up — for one player or everyone — and can gift a talent.
- **The player** spends the level: talents, attributes, abilities.
- **The app** adds it up, checks what each talent needs, and applies the automatic talents to the sheet.

**How it connects.**
- *Fed by:* missions · fights · Spotlight (often a Drive acted on at a cost).
- *Feeds:* attributes and skills (12-2) · abilities and the class engine · talents (Cover Habit, Keen Edge…).

**Say it at the table.**
> "You earn XP for the job, the danger and the moments that were yours. Every 10 is a level, and you spend it when I open level-up — usually between missions."

**Where to look.** [5-9, progress](#/manual/sec-5-9). The GM's side: [9-11](#/manual/sec-9-11).

## 12-17. Chases {#sec-12-17}

**What it's for.** Not every encounter is a fight. Sometimes the crew runs — or chases — through a city too big and too crowded for a tactical map. A chase is about distance and choices under pressure, not damage.

**The rule in one line.** A gap from 0 (caught) to 6 (lost them); each leg everyone makes one move and one roll, half the crew must keep pace, the pursuers roll once, and the gap moves.

**Who decides.**
- **The GM** sets it up (who chases — a starter or a saved preset — and the starting gap), frames each stretch, keeps or redraws its complication, chooses whether the pursuers chase or stop and shoot, rolls for them, and decides what each cost is: the complication, their fire, the tiring, leaving someone behind, stopping for a fight.
- **The players** pick their move each leg — run, lead, give cover fire, hack the drone or the autopilots, pull a trick, help, hide — and roll it on their sheets.
- **The app** moves the gap by the rules, tracks who failed and how often, and puts every cost on a button — never applies it by itself.

**How it connects.**
- *Uses:* group checks (12-2) for keeping pace · help (12-2) · weapons and ammo (Cover fire) · hacking (12-9) — a drone overhead means no hiding until it's down · ⚡ Harm (12-11) for every complication and every shot · Strain (12-12) and CON from leg 5.
- *Ends in:* a fight on the map (12-7: Prepare) when caught or stopped; nothing when they get away.
- *Later:* vehicles — robotaxis with hackable autopilots, manual driving as a rare skill — on the same track.

**Say it at the table.**
> "They're after you. Each stretch, tell me what you do — run, find a way, slow them down, kill their drone, hide — and roll it. If half of you keep up, you hold the gap or open it. Six and you're gone; zero and they've got you. And the longer it goes on, the more it costs."

**Where to look.** [6-9, Chases](#/manual/sec-6-9). The GM's side: [9-4](#/manual/sec-9-4) — *🏃 Chase*.

## 12-18. The case: dossier and board {#sec-12-18}

**What it's for.** A job is a pile of fragments — an objective, a photo, a name, a code on a receipt — and each player tends to hold a different one. The case puts them in one place, per mission, so the crew can reason together instead of reconstructing what happened.

**The rule in one line.** The dossier is what the crew knows for sure (the GM keeps it); the board is what they make of it (one player holds the pen at a time).

**Who decides.**
- **The GM** keeps the dossier: which secondary objectives the crew knows about, which people and places are revealed; what's sent to everyone goes in by itself. The GM can change the board too, and pins anonymous tips.
- **The players** build the board: cards, links, a picture from the dossier; whoever holds the pen edits, the others suggest; they pass the pen among themselves.
- **The app** files things by mission, keeps every card (unpinned ones go to a drawer), and never decides what a clue means.

**How it connects.**
- *Fed by:* objectives (Story tab) · handouts, puzzles and messages sent to everyone · revealed people and places · whatever the crew writes.
- *Feeds:* the crew's planning before a job and between scenes · a recap at the start of a session · puzzles (the code was on the board all along).

**Say it at the table.**
> "Everything you know for sure is in the dossier on your Case tab — objectives, pictures, names. The board is yours: one of you holds the pen and pins things up, the rest of you suggest. Pass the pen when you like."

**Where to look.** [6-10, The case](#/manual/sec-6-10). The GM's side: [9-9](#/manual/sec-9-9).

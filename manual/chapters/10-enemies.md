# Enemies {#ch-10}

## 10. The collection

Every crew ends up across a corridor from someone. This chapter is a field guide to who that might be: street muggers and junk drones, Watch patrols, syndicate muscle, the Obsidian TRF squads nobody wants to meet, and the named few who earn a page of their own.

It is a **collection**, not a rulebook. Browse it to get a feel for the street. The GM's own library is the source: names change, numbers move, new faces appear and some of these will be rebuilt for a particular job. At the table you never see these cards. Your sheet shows a letter, the name the GM gives (*Guard in red*), whether the target is organic or a machine, and how hurt it looks. Knowing this chapter by heart tells you what *could* be in the room, never what *is*.

## 10-1. Reading a card {#sec-10-1}

A card here shows a type and what it looks like. **The numbers below are not printed**: your sheet shows them once your character knows that type — *Intel* (7-9). This list tells you what they mean when you do.

- **Type** — *organic* (flesh, maybe a little chrome), *cybernetic* (people with networked implants) or *synthetic* (drones, robots, turrets). It decides what a Hacker can touch (most hacks only reach cybernetic and synthetic targets) and what a location hit does (10-3).
- **HP** — how much damage it takes to drop it. Players see only the wound band: *Unhurt*, *Hurt*, *Bloodied* (half HP or less), *Down*.
- **Armor** — subtracted from the damage of every hit, exactly like yours. It cracks by 1 when it stops a location attack completely (7-5).
- **Actions** — actions per round, spent like yours (move, attack, reload, take cover…).
- **Squares** — squares per Move action.
- **INIT** — added to its d20 for the turn order.
- **Attacks** — name, type (*melee* reaches adjacent squares; *ranged*; *thrown*), the **TN** it rolls under on a d20, and its damage. The number after ⌖ is the TN for a **location attack** (−4).
- **Behaviour** — how it fights. This is the GM's guide to playing it: a reckless mugger charges, a sniper stays put, a drone holds the corridor.
- **Special** — a rule only this enemy has. Regular enemies have none; their behaviour is their trick. Most elites have one; a Special marked *story only* is flavour, already counted in the numbers.

## 10-2. How enemies fight {#sec-10-2}

**The rule.** Enemies follow the players' rules. They roll a d20 under their attack TN; on a hit the target's sheet subtracts its own armor and cover. A location attack costs −4 TN and adds the location's condition if damage gets through. Enemies don't track ammo and have no class engine. They take conditions like anyone else: a Slowed guard loses an action, a Stunned one stands there.

**What isn't on the card.** Whether an enemy **sees in the dark** depends on what it carries tonight, not on its kind. The GM decides per fight (night vision, thermal, or nothing). Obsidian's TRF squads usually do; a Watch patrolman rarely does (7-2 §4).

**How it plays.** Most enemies on the street aren't paid enough to die. A habit, not a rule: regular enemies think about running once they're *Bloodied*, when their leader drops, or when the fight turns obviously against them. They surrender, flee, or call for backup that may arrive in two rounds. Fanatics and machines don't. Elites decide for themselves, and the good ones come back.

**In the GM console (Combat tab).**

- **Add enemies** from the library: search, choose how many copies, *Add*. Or type a custom one on the last line.
- **Players see** — give each a public name; *Hide* keeps an ambusher off the players' screens until revealed.
- **Attack buttons** — a normal attack or ⌖ a location attack; the console rolls, then *Send hit* to the target's sheet.
- **Damage taken** — type the damage, pick **where** (normal hit, or ⌖ head / chest / abdomen / arm / leg) and *Apply*. Armor is subtracted (unless you tick *ignore armor*), and the location adds its condition by itself.
- **Sees in the dark** — set it per enemy when the lights go out.

## 10-3. Location attacks against enemies {#sec-10-3}

**The rule.** The same as against an Operator (7-5): declare it before rolling, **−4 TN**; on a hit roll a d20 for the location (Aim shifts it by up to ±5), roll damage as usual. If any damage gets through the armor, the location adds its condition; if the armor stops everything, the armor cracks by 1.

- **Organic and cybernetic targets** use the body table below: legs *Crippled leg*, arms *Broken arm*, abdomen *Prone*, chest *Slowed*, head *Stunned* — or *Incapacitated* if the hit leaves it at half HP or less.
- **Synthetic targets** map differently: legs and tracks *Crippled leg*, a weapon mount (arm) *Jammed* (no attacks until it spends an action to clear it), chest and abdomen *Stunned*, sensors (head) *Blinded*.
- **Immune** — some elites shrug off a condition (they can't be knocked prone, say). The hit still deals its damage.

::table[hit-locations]

> *Example:* a Watch Patrolman (25 HP, armor 3) holds a doorway. The Soldier declares a location attack: TN 13 becomes 9, and she rolls 7 — a hit. The location d20 is 16: chest. Damage 7, minus 3 armor: 4 gets through. The patrolman drops to 21 HP and is **Slowed** — one action fewer on his next turn. Later the Hacker's location shot hits a Junk Drone's arm for 2 against its armor 1: 1 gets through, and the drone's taser mount is **Jammed**.

## 10-4. Regular enemies {#sec-10-4}

The people and machines a crew meets most: from the alley to the corporate floor, roughly in rising order of trouble. Regular enemies come in groups. They are dangerous in numbers and through the tactics the GM gives them, not because of any trick of their own.

::table[enemies]

## 10-5. Elites {#sec-10-5}

Elites have names, more HP, two attacks and usually a **Special** rule that changes how the fight plays. One is plenty for a scene. (Elite abilities will grow into a fuller system later; for now each has at most one simple Special.) When a Special changes a player's numbers (a −2 TN aura, ignored cover), the GM says so before the roll and folds it into that player's difficulty step; the player doesn't need to know where it came from.

::table[elites]

> **Keep it fun.** An elite is a character, not a damage sponge. Let them talk, threaten, bargain, retreat with a promise to return. A fight with an elite the players already hate is worth three with strangers.

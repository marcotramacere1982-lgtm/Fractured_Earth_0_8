# Running the Game {#ch-9}

## 9. Behind the screen

This chapter is for the Game Master. It covers the rules that are yours to apply, how they play out at the table, and where each one lives in the **GM console** (the `/gm/` page the table server opens for you). Each section follows the same order: **the rule**, **how it plays**, **in the console**. Where the two are the same thing, they are merged.

The app is a smart paper sheet for everyone, the GM included. It does the maths and remembers what happened; it does not run the game. Every number it shows can be overridden by you, and every tool in the console that changes a sheet can be taken back.

## 9-1. Fun first {#sec-9-1}

**The rule.** The GM's job is a good evening at the table, not a correct simulation. The rules exist to make choices matter and outcomes surprising. When a rule gets in the way of that, you change the ruling and keep playing. Players should never feel the app is the referee: you are.

**How it plays.** Watch the table, not only the screen. Signs a scene isn't working:

- phones out, side conversations, the same player talking for twenty minutes;
- a fight past round four where nothing has changed but the HP numbers;
- three failed rolls on the same door with nothing new learned;
- a player who has been quiet all evening.

Most of the time the problem is the scene, not the dice. The encounter you built may be too long, too flat or too hard. Change it while it runs:

- **End a fight that is decided.** Enemies surrender, flee, call for a truce or simply break. Narrate the mop-up instead of rolling it.
- **Collapse a chain of rolls into one.** A whole lock, a whole climb, a whole search: one check, and the margin says how well it went.
- **Fail forward.** A failure still moves the story: the door opens but the alarm trips, the clue is found but someone saw you (6-3).
- **Move the difficulty.** Easier tiers exist for this (9-4): help, time, good tools.
- **Hand someone the spotlight.** Ask the quiet player what their character notices; the Explore tab tells you what they are good at (9-5).

**Overrides are yours.** Every one of these is in the console, and none of them needs an excuse:

- **Difficulty** for one roll or the whole table (9-4).
- **Your own dice and notes**, outside the rules (*🎲 Roll / note*, 9-4).
- **Undo** a player's action, a round or a whole fight (9-14).
- **Accept or revert** changes to a sheet (9-3).
- **Gift** talents, XP, items and unique gear (9-11, 9-13).
- **Hide, reveal, move** anything on the map (9-8).

> **Keep it fun — what this does not mean.** It does not mean rerolling every failure or saving every character. Failure, cost and loss are what make success feel earned, and players notice when nothing can go wrong. Use overrides to fix *the evening* (a scene that drags, a rule that misfired, a mistake with a button), not to remove risk. A good test: would the table thank you for it if they saw you do it?

## 9-2. Opening the table {#sec-9-2}

**How it works.** The live table runs from one computer on the room's Wi-Fi. `npm run table` starts the server and prints two things: the **address** players open on their tablets and the **GM key**. Open `/gm/` on your laptop and type the key. Nothing needs internet.

Players open the address, pick their name under **At the table · Play as** on the characters page and open a character: that joins the table with it (the Dossier's **Join the table** still works too). Every character they create, import or open while playing as themselves is kept by the table under their name, so it follows them to any tablet; when two devices played the same one, the newest save wins and the history keeps the rest. **Admin → Players** lists every player and their characters: rename, merge two entries for the same person, move a character to the right player, remove an empty name. From then on their sheet sends its state to you after every action, and receives your messages, difficulty, light, combat order and map.

**In the console — Table tab.**

- **Player cards** — one per connected character: HP, armor, conditions and injuries, the class engine, readied weapons, the round's actions, level and XP, cash and CRS, the debt, the last log lines.
- **Message a player** — the field at the bottom of each card. Messages wait in a mailbox until the sheet has shown them, so a tablet that was asleep still gets them.
- **Tell everyone** — the bar at the top; *Refresh* asks every sheet to send its state again.
- **Sit out** — hides a character and keeps it out of fights (a player who joined with the wrong sheet, someone who left early). It goes to **Not playing now**, with offline characters; *Bring back* returns it.
- **Table log** — every action, roll and purchase as it happens. The 📌 in the Story tab pins lines into the session log (9-9).
- **Whispers** — players write to you privately from their sheet (✉ GM in the sheet's bar, while at the table). They land in the Table log marked *✉ to you* with a toast; *↩ Reply* jumps to that player's message field. Only you see them; they're kept on disk, so a reloaded console still has them.
- **On a player card** — *↺ Refill* (in a fight) gives back the round's actions and clears borrowed ones, for a mistake or a ruling; *+ XP* grants extra XP with a reason that goes in their log. A player can sit at the table with one character at a time: opening a second one asks, and the first leaves with a notice. Renaming an enemy (*Players see*) gives it the initials of its new name on the tracker, the map and the players' cards.

Sheets play a soft sound when a player's turn starts, when you send something and when they are hit. Players switch it off on their own device.

## 9-3. The sheet watch {#sec-9-3}

**The rule.** Players own their sheets, and a browser can't stop anyone editing one. So the console doesn't block changes; it **notices** them.

**How it plays.** Changes from play — HP, ammo, conditions, class resources, notes — always pass. Changes to the **foundation** — attributes, CRS, cash, inventory, credentials, class, XP, talents — are checked against the sheet's own log. A purchase explains the cash it cost; a gift explains the item. What the log doesn't explain is **flagged**.

**In the console.** A flagged card shows **Unexplained changes**: what changed, from what to what. *Accept* makes it the new baseline; *Revert* sends the old value back to the sheet. Every snapshot is kept on disk (`local/table/history/`), so nothing is ever lost.

> **Keep it fun.** A flag is almost always a mistake — a typo in an attribute, a test purchase, a player fixing something you told them to fix. Ask first, out loud and kindly. The watch is there so nobody has to wonder, not to catch anyone.

## 9-4. Checks, difficulty and light {#sec-9-4}

**The rule.** Every check is a d20 rolled **under** a Target Number (TN): the skill or attribute TN, changed by one **difficulty step** for the whole situation (6-2). Harder steps lower the TN; the easier ones — **Favourable +2, Easy +4, Very easy +6** — raise it. Help is not a separate rule: one helper is Favourable, two people giving each other a leg-up is Easy, the whole crew with the right gear is Very easy. One step, never stacked, and only the person acting rolls. Missing by 1 can be a success with a cost; a natural 1 or 20 adds something the GM narrates. Contested and passive checks are in 6-2.

**How it plays.** Call for a roll only when failure would be interesting. Opening an unlocked door, buying lunch, knowing what your own career taught you: no roll. When you do call for one, say the step out loud, or set it for the table.

The sheet already counts what the player carries: tools appear as tick boxes (one counts), active items and stims show as *In play*, and in the dark a *Light* row suggests the right line (7-2 §4). You have the last word on all three: "the lockpick doesn't help with a keycard" is your call.

**In the console — Table tab, top row.**

- **Table difficulty** — Off, or a step from *Very easy +6* to the hardest. It appears on every sheet as a banner and is pre-picked in every roll window until you switch it off. For a single roll, just tell the player which step to pick.
- **Light** — *Bright* / *Dark* for checks when there is no map on the table. With a map, each player's own square decides (9-8).
- **🎲 Roll / note** (console header) — your own dice and notes, outside the rules: any expression (`3d6+2`), an optional TN to roll under, *About* (a player or an NPC) and a note. *Log* writes one line into the live mission's session log. Use it for secret rolls, NPC checks, random tables, or just to remember something said at the table. Nothing reaches the sheets.
- **🎬 New scene** (console header) — when the scene changes (a new place, time passes, the fight is long over), tap it: on every sheet, scene-long item effects end (no crash), toy drones recharge and once-per-scene abilities (*Scrounge*) come back; the session log gets a *New scene* line. It asks first.
- **⚡ Event…** (in the same window) — for when the table stalls: 30 short events (tension, clue, social, hazard, opportunity, and the Hum), searchable and filtered by kind; the ones you used lately sink to the bottom. Pick one and read it out (or make it yours); each comes with 1–3 checks, the best- and worst-placed classes, and a twist. Tap a check: the dice become 1d20 and, if *About* is a character at the table, the TN is theirs with the difficulty applied. *Log* writes the event, the roll and the matching outcome into the session log. You choose, you narrate — nothing is sent to the players.

> **Keep it fun.** If the whole plan hangs on one roll, either make it easier or make failure open a different door. A heist that dies on a Sleight of Hand check at the front gate is a short evening.

## 9-5. Exploration and the spotlight {#sec-9-5}

**The rule.** Out of combat the game runs on intent and means (6-3): the player says what they want and how; you name the skill and the step; the dice decide how well. Time is a resource (the Operating Clock, 6-1); preparation before a job (the zero-round, 6-4) earns advantages for the fight to come. Locks follow 5-3: mechanical → Sleight of Hand, electronic → Electronics / Security, networked → Computers / Hacking, or STR to force it.

**In the console — Explore tab.** A cheat sheet for everything that isn't a fight:

- **Ask for a roll** — pick a skill or attribute (or tap its row in *Who's best at…*), tick one player or a group, add a word on why if you like, *Ask*. Each sheet gets a card with *Roll*: the usual roll window, with your table difficulty, their tools and the light. The result comes back under your ask (✓ / ✗, the die against the TN) and as a pop-up; a player can put a card aside (✕) without rolling.
- **Who's best at…** — every skill side by side for the characters playing now, the best TN highlighted. When nobody knows who should try, you do.
- **Per character** — debt anchor, notable traits, credentials, life path and backstory. These are your hooks: a contact from their career, a creditor from their debt, a place from their youth.
- **Look and portrait** — the player's *Appearance & manner* sits under the photo (read only: it is theirs). **📷 Change** sends a new portrait straight to that sheet (it waits if the player is offline). At the bottom, *Prompts* holds the portrait and backstory prompts with a Copy button: players without an image AI can leave the picture to you. Each card shows name, class, HP, Drive and strongest skills; everything else folds (appearance, anchors, life path, backstory, prompts) and stays as you left it.

> **Keep it fun.** Give each player a moment where *their* character is the right one for the job. The Explore tab makes it easy to see who hasn't had one yet.

## 9-6. Running a fight {#sec-9-6}

**The rule.** Everyone rolls **d20 + INIT** once; the order stays for the whole fight (7-1). A combatant may *Delay* to the end of the round. Each turn has a number of actions (shown on every sheet and enemy card): move, attack, ability, reload, interact, aim, and so on (7-3). Enemy attacks are rolled exactly like the players': d20 under the attack TN, damage minus the target's armor, location attacks at −4 TN with a condition if damage gets through (7-5). Players never see enemy stats: their sheets show an anonymous order and the **Hostiles** list — letter, the name you give, a wound band (Unhurt, Hurt, Bloodied, Down) and visible conditions.

**In the console — Combat tab.**

1. **Set up.** *New encounter*. Search the enemy library (chapter 10 shows it as a collection), choose how many copies and *Add*; or fill the custom line (name, HP, armor, actions, INIT, one attack). Connected players who aren't sitting out join automatically. **Pictures:** an enemy with art shows a thumbnail in the order and its portrait on its card (tap it for the full picture); copies wear different looks where there are several — *◐ Look* changes one. *🤝 Ally* puts an entry on the crew's side: the sheets list it by name, in green, under *Allies*, and the challenge meter stops counting it. Players see a picture only while that enemy is on the board. The art is GM-only: drop `<id>_portrait[_n]` / `<id>_full[_n]` images into `assets/enemies_raw/` and run `node tools/enemy-art.mjs` (it writes small WebP copies to `local/gm/art/`).
2. **Initiative.** *Roll missing initiative* rolls for everyone without a roll; type the d20 for players who rolled at the table. *Re-roll all* if you need to start over. *Ask players to roll* puts an Initiative card on the sheet of every player without a roll; their die lands in the setup as they answer (*rolling…* until then).
3. **Start combat.** Sheets get the order and their turn banner.
4. **Turns.** *Now acting* shows whose turn it is. Mark each combatant *Done* (in any order); *Delay* moves one to the end of the round; *Next turn ▶* moves on. **Player hits:** when a player attacks an enemy they picked, the hit waits at the top of the enemy column, prefilled (who, damage, location) and editable: *Apply* runs it through that enemy's armor and location like damage you type; *Dismiss* drops it. Misses show for a moment as news. Abilities land here too: a pending hit can carry conditions (ticked: Suppressed, Marked, Prone…), armor cracks and the armor rule (half / ignored), and *Apply* adds them; an ability that only needs you to do something (cover, a decoy, a reveal) shows as a note with *Done*.
5. **End round.** Runs every sheet's round end: class engines tick, timed effects count down, conditions clear.
6. **End combat** when it's over. *Clear defeated* tidies the list mid-fight.

**Enemy cards.**

- **Players see** — the public name ("Guard in red"); the letter is given automatically and never reused. *Hide* keeps an enemy (an ambusher, a sniper) off the players' order and Hostiles list until you reveal it.
- **HP** — type the damage a player tells you and pick where it landed (a normal hit, or ⌖ head / chest / abdomen / arm / leg for a location attack: the condition is added for you); armor is subtracted unless you tick *ignore armor*. *Crack armor −1* when a location attack is fully stopped.
- **ACT** pips track the enemy's actions this round.
- **Attack buttons** — normal or ⌖ location attack open a **roll window**: pick who it attacks and body or location, then each die — *🎲 Roll* or type the die you rolled (*Use*): the d20, then on a hit the damage and, for a location attack, where it lands (with what it does). The first d20 uses one of the enemy's actions. *Send the hit* puts a card on that player's sheet: what hits them and what gets through their armor, with *Apply* or *Dodge*; the combat log tells you which they chose. A miss is just logged. An enemy's conditions change its numbers: its attack buttons and the window show the TN its own conditions leave it (Blinded −10, Suppressed −4, Focused +2), plus the target's (melee at a Prone player +4, ranged at a Blinded one +4); Enraged adds +2 to its melee damage; Slowed and Stunned cut its action pips like a player's.
- **Injuries and conditions** — tap to set; they show on the players' Hostiles list.
- **Sees in the dark** — night vision or thermal: the dark doesn't hide anyone from it (7-2 §4).
- **Notes** — position, target, tricks. A Scavenger's **Tells** on that enemy show here too.

**The challenge meter** estimates how hard the fight is from both sides' damage per round, armor and HP: **Easy, Fair, Hard, Deadly**, with notes ("armor nobody can get through", "one-round knockout risk"). It ignores abilities, cover and tactics, so treat it as a first opinion. The rating when the fight starts also decides the danger XP (9-11).

**↶ Undo** on the tracker takes back your last changes (about 15) and is announced to the table as "The GM took back their last move" — no names, so hidden enemies stay hidden.

> **Keep it fun.** The meter can't see the room. If a Deadly fight is a slog after two rounds, the enemies' morale breaks; if an Easy one is over in a turn and the players look bored, reinforcements arrive. A fight should end when its question is answered — who controls the room, did they get the data — not when the last HP is gone.

## 9-7. Building maps {#sec-9-7}

**The rule.** Tactical maps use squares of **1.5 m** (7-2). Walls and closed doors block movement and sight; open doors and windows let sight through; pieces can block sight, give cover or make difficult ground (2 squares per square).

**In the console — Maps tab.** A builder for the maps you'll put on the table: the map and the library on the left, the drawing in the middle, and on the right **Properties** — whatever you selected (or the tool's options), then your blocks and saved maps. Properties set how things **start** on the table: a door already locked, a light off, a piece hidden, a weak wall already broken or showing cracks. The Deploy tab changes them live; the saved map keeps the start.

- **The toolbar** is a row of icons (names and keys on hover): select, erase, pan, zoom, undo · group · walls (thin, standard, thick), **glass wall**, door, window, laser · prop, zone · snap **1** (whole squares), **½** (half squares) or **·** (free; G cycles) · save, new, duplicate · **Bright / Dark** for the map.
- **Walls** in three widths, **doors** and **windows** on the grid lines. A door can start open, closed or **locked** (locks are never shown to players). A **glass wall** (offices, shop fronts) blocks movement but not sight and gives no cover; it starts breakable, so the crew can smash through it on your call.
- **Breakable walls** — select a wall and tick *Breakable*: a weak spot the crew can break through on your call. Only you see it (dashed amber); players see an ordinary wall until you show its cracks on the Deploy tab (9-8).
- **Floors** (decision 184) — under the map's size: *2 side by side* / *3 side by side* split the map into floors with one empty column between, each in a dashed frame with its name (rename it, nudge its x / y / w / h); *None* goes back to one floor. Draw each floor in its frame, then select a staircase or lift and pick **Leads to** — the piece on another floor it reaches. On the table nobody sees or walks across floors; a linked pair costs 2 squares to cross, and the fog of war keeps the other floor dark until someone goes up.

Vehicles block movement and give half cover; vans and box trucks also block sight.
- **Caption** — what players read when they tap the piece once you've revealed it ("Precinct 14"): pins on a city or district map, a shop sign, a name on a door.
- **Label & note** — any selected piece can carry a GM-only label and note ("Getaway car — keys in the ignition"). Players never see them; tap the piece on the Deploy tab to read them.
- **Event zones** — the *Zone* tool drags a rectangle: **Entry**, **Trigger** (numbered T1, T2…), **Exit** or **Note**, each with a label and a note ("patrol arrives from the east stairwell"). Only you see them and they change nothing in the rules: tap one on the Deploy tab to read its note.
- **Several at once & the clipboard** — with *Select*, **Shift + drag** draws a box round everything you want (it switches to the Group tool); **Ctrl + click** adds a piece, wall or zone to the selection, or takes it out; **Alt + click** only takes out. **Hold the mouse wheel** (or, in the builder, **hold Space** — handy on a touchpad) and drag to pan from any tool (the Deploy map and the players' map too); let go and you're back where you were. **Esc** ends a run of walls, then leaves whatever you were placing (pieces, blocks, a paste, zones…) for **Select** — the builder's default — and a further Esc clears the selection. **Ctrl+C** copies the box or the one selected thing, **Ctrl+X** cuts, **Ctrl+V** pastes: tap where it goes, as many times as you like, R turns it, Esc stops — it works across maps. The **floor lock** (toolbar, or F) makes floor tiles unselectable: boxes, taps and the eraser skip them, so you copy a room's furniture without its floor (drains, stains and manholes stay selectable).
- **Groups and blocks** — the *Group* tool drags a box round walls, pieces and zones: drag inside it to move them together, or *Turn*, *Duplicate*, *Delete*. *Save as block* keeps the group by name (a motel room, a row of cubicles); pick it in **Blocks** and tap to drop copies, turned with R. What you drop is independent — edit each copy as you like. Blocks are kept in `local/gm/blocks.json`.
- **Save**, *Duplicate*, *Export file*. Maps are saved in `local/gm/maps/`; nothing is published.
- **Folders & 📁 Library** — a folder is the start of a name: call a map or a block *Motel / Single room* and it is listed under **Motel** in the dropdowns. **📁 Library** (beside Blocks and Saved maps) shows maps or blocks by folder with a small preview, a search, and *Open* / *Place*, *Rename*, *Folder…*, *Duplicate* and *Remove* (removed maps go to `removed/`, nothing is lost).
- **Layers** — top of the right column: Floors, Walls & openings, Pieces, Lights & power, Sensors & lasers, Links, Zones, each 👁 shown, ◐ dimmed (faded, can't be clicked) or ✕ hidden; tap a layer's name to see only it. Handy to pick a desk without grabbing the floor, or to check the lights on their own. Only the builder's view: nothing is removed, the table never sees it.
- **Facilities** — six specialised places in the library: **Server farm** (rack rows, network core, cooling units, raised floor and its hatches, operations desk, screen wall, UPS and battery strings — power sources —, gas suppression, tape robot, a mantrap), **Power plant** (turbine, generator set, a micro-reactor, transformer and switchgear you can trip, control console, mimic panel, pumps, pipes and valves, fuel tank, shield blocks, a dosimeter gate, grid batteries), **Hospital** (beds, curtains, monitors, operating table and surgical light, a surgical robot, crash cart, pharmacy dispenser, triage, gurney, morgue, decontamination shower, CT scanner, medical drone dock), **Garage & repair** (car lift, inspection pit, tool wall, engine hoist, EV charger, battery swap bay, paint booth, a car on stands, welding screen, drone bench), **Drone factory** (robot cell, gantry, print farm, test cage, drone racks and crates, charging wall, clean-room airlock, launch rail, parked drones — conveyors, robot arms and 3D printers are under Industrial) and **Barracks & training** (footlockers, weapon racks, armory cage, firing lane and pop-up targets, sandbags, climbing wall, crawl net, tyre run, kill-house walls, mess table, lockers, a VR sim pod — bunks are under Front offices). New type tags #medical and #weapons; the power pieces are ready to wire.
- **Power wiring** (GM only) — the *Wire* tool (X): tap a **source** (generator, substation, hacked tap, pylon, battery bank), **breaker** (breaker panel, fusebox, junction box), **switch** or **socket**, then everything it feeds — lights, sensors, keypads, screens, sockets, other breakers, **electric doors** (tick *Electric door* on a selected door and choose *Fail-secure*: locks when the power goes, or *Fail-safe*: unlocks). Tap the start again or Esc to stop; Alt + click removes a wire, the Eraser too. A breaker nothing is wired into is **the mains**: the circuit starts there (wire a generator into it and it depends on the generator instead). A **power box** is a flat's or a floor's local board: it is never the mains — wire it from the building's junction box or breaker panel, then the room's switches and sockets from it; trip either board and the room goes dark. Trip it, cut its source or a cable, and everything downstream goes dark. Tags S1, F1, SW1, O1, D1 name them; a **!** marks a light, sensor, switch or socket no wire reaches. A piece is fed while any wire into it comes from something fed, so a backup generator on the same breaker keeps it live until both are cut. *Battery* (some pieces have one: emergency lamps, alarm keypads, fire alarms, vault panels; tick it on any other) keeps it working without power. Unwired pieces stay manual, as before. Wires show on the *Lights & power* layer and never reach players.
- **Data network** (GM only) — a **network uplink** (a fibre point, or a server farm's core cabinet; there can be more than one) starts a network; **routers** pass it along cables; **Wi-Fi access points** and **repeaters** pass it over the air (a faint ring shows their range). Devices are *Wi-Fi only* (cheap sensors, older cameras, fire alarms) or *cable or Wi-Fi* (360° and dome cameras, keypads, panels, racks, consoles). The *Wire* tool has three modes: *Power*, *Data cable*, *Wi-Fi link*. With one network you rarely tap: a Wi-Fi device in range joins the nearest access point by itself (a dotted line); draw a Wi-Fi link only to choose between two networks. Tags N1, R1, W1, RP1; a **?** marks a networked device that reaches no network. Routers and access points need power (wire them); every security device has a backup battery. The *Network* layer shows it all.
- **Region maps** — *Maps at scale → Region* in the library: simple ground squares (flat, hills, mountains, forest, desert, wasteland, sea, lake, river and river bend) and coloured octagon **nodes** — cities in three sizes, grid node, exchange node, port, airport, water treatment, reactor, refinery, data relay, corporate enclave, Grey Zone settlement, checkpoint, Watch base, farms, mine, ruin and an unknown “?”. Give each node a **caption** (its name). The **Link** tool (key I) draws the connections between them on the grid lines: **People** (solid), **Goods** (dashed), **Power** (dotted, yellow), **Data** (dash-dot, cyan), **Water / fuel** (double, blue). Several links can share a line: each kind runs a little to one side. A link blocks nothing; it can start hidden, or be cut. Use ½ snap to run links between node centres. No distances, no travel times: the map shows what is connected, you tell how long it takes.
- **City maps** — the whole city, a square being a district. *Maps at scale → City* in the library: **districts** that fill their square (downtown core and mixed towers, business district, a 2×2 arcology, dense housing, tenement belt, residential, suburbs, old town, slum sprawl, Grey Zone, bazaar quarter, light and heavy industry, power station, construction site, civic centre, hospital & university campus, Watch fortress, rail yard, airport, port, city park, stadium, necropolis, ruined district), **water** (sea, shore and its corner, river and bend — rivers lie over a district tile) and **highways, rail & walls** laid over any tile (highway straight, curve, junction and interchange, rail, maglev, cordon wall). Links run over all of these. Nothing blocks movement or sight at this scale: reveal districts by hand with fog, or play with fog off. Each district has a height (open, low, mid, high, towers): **Skyline preview** (the city-blocks icon in the bar, only on maps with city tiles) shows the city at an angle, buildings rising from every district — look only; tap a district to select it and change its **Height** in Properties; Esc goes back to top-down. The chain: region → city → district → the action map, and you decide when to open the next one.
- **District maps** — a part of the city seen from above, a square being a plot of 10–15 m. *Maps at scale → District* in the library: **streets** (two-lane streets, two-square avenues with a planted median and zebra crossings, alleys — each straight, corner, T, crossroads or dead end — plaza paving, pedestrian street, rail line and crossing, canal and bridge, empty lot), **homes** (courtyard and U blocks, slabs, tenement megablock and tower, row houses, detached houses, slum ghettos, corporate housing), **civic & security** (city hall, school, hospital, clinic, credit & debt bureau, Watch precinct, fire station, checkpoint), **corporate** (towers, skyscrapers, an arcology, office block, data centre), **industry & utilities**, **everyday life** (church, chapel, market hall, street market, ration depot, shops, bar), **transport** (station, metro, parking, drone port), **parks & sport** and **landmarks** (monument, statue, column, obelisk, fountain, AR tower). Buildings block movement and sight, so with fog *On* the crew sees the street they walk and what lines it — an unmapped slum; with fog *Off* it is a district they know. Slum ghettos can be walked (slowly) but not seen through; parks, plazas, parking lots, markets and landmarks are open ground. Set these maps to *bright*: light rules don't work at this scale. Give buildings a caption ("St. Agnes", "Precinct 14").

> **Keep it fun.** A map is a stage, not a puzzle. Build the rooms where something will happen, and describe the rest. Ten minutes on a good map beats an hour on a perfect one.

## 9-8. The map on the table {#sec-9-8}

**The rule.** Movement, line of sight and range follow 7-2. The map *informs*; you *decide*. Line of sight and range shown on a tablet are a guide — you confirm cover and anything unusual.

**How it plays.** Players see an **Active map** tab while a map is on the table (7-2 §5). They see what their crew can see right now, and remember what they've seen. They drag their own marker: free out of combat; in combat one Move action gives them their squares for the round. They open and close doors next to them (an Interact action in combat). A locked door stays shut: they're told it's locked, you get a note that they tried. Torches, flares and vision gear work on their own.

**In the console — Deploy tab.**

- **Put a map on the table** — pick a saved map; the turn tracker sits beside it.
- **Not on the map** — players and enemies waiting to be placed: tap one, then a square. Drag markers to move them; tap one marker then another to check sight.
- **Players move their markers: on / off** — let players drag their own markers, or move everyone yourself. In combat the console re-checks every move against the player's squares and turn.
- **Fog of war & light** — fog *On* or *Off — they see everything*; *Reveal all*, *Reset*, and *Edit squares* to reveal a square by hand (a floor plan they bought, a camera feed). *Bright* / *Dark* changes the environment live.
- **Pieces** — tap one to reveal it, hide it again, switch it on or off (lights, sensors, lasers), open or lock a door, wreck a vehicle (or make it intact), **✥ Move** it (then tap the square it goes to) or turn it ⟲ ⟳ — a car pulling up, a barricade dragged across the alley — or take it off for this session. Its GM label and note show here too, and you can edit them. The saved map keeps its starting state.
- **Weak walls** — tap a breakable wall: *Break through* turns it into a breach for everyone (nothing blocks movement or sight there; *Repair* if you tapped the wrong one), and *Show cracks to players* hints it on their map when a character notices. The roll to break it is your call.
- **Event zones** — tap one to read its label and note.
- **NPCs** — pick a role (worker, clerk, guard…) and *+ Add*: players see *Worker 1* until you rename it to a real name. Each NPC has a private note for you.
- **Marks** — players point things out to each other in their colour (a crosshair, an arrow, a question mark, a freehand line, 7-2 §5); they fade after two minutes unless pinned. Pick a tool to draw your own, in amber ("you hear it from *here*"); in marking mode a tap on any mark removes it; *Clear all* wipes the board. Marks are never saved and never touch the rules; they go when the map comes off the table.
- **Table state** — when you take a map off the table (or put another one on), it remembers how it looked: revealed pins and captions, doors, breaches, moved or wrecked pieces, lights, every NPC and **Group** marker, the fog. Put it back later and choose **Resume** (as they left it) or **Start fresh** from the saved map; *Save table state* keeps it at any moment. Players who aren't here aren't drawn; their spots come back when they are. Handy for a city or district map the crew keeps returning to: move a **Group** marker (an NPC role: *Group A — Kira & Jordan*) when the party splits.
- **The TV screen** — the table server prints a TV address ending in **/screen**. Open it in a browser tab and cast that tab to the TV: a bar on top and the map full screen, as the crew sees it together — every player's marker, what any of them sees, bright or dark, everyone's marks. It only watches: nothing on it changes the table, and it never shows messages, the fight's numbers or anything you keep hidden. Pan and zoom it like any map; *Fit*, *Full screen* and *Day / Night* sit in its bar (the other buttons are for later). It follows whatever map is on the table.
- **Links** — tap a link: *Reveal* / *Hide* it, *Cut it* (players see a red cross on it: the power line is down, the road is blocked) or *Restore it*. Players' Map tab lists the kinds of link on the map.
- **Power** — tap a breaker: *Trip* / *Reset*; a switch: *Switch off* / *on*; a source: *Cut the power* / *Restore power*. You're told first what it does ("Turns off: Ceiling light ×3 · Locks: door D1"). A wired piece or electric door lists the cables into it, each with *Cut the cable* / *Repair the cable*, and says whether it has power. Lights go dark for everyone, sensors stop, electric doors lock or unlock by their mode; players only see what happens.
- **Security board** (left column, on maps with a network or security devices) — what the security room sees right now: each node and device *reporting*, *offline* (and where it was cut), *fooled* (false data), *no power* or *not networked*, with how long ("since round 3" in a fight). Tap one to act on it: **Take offline / Bring online** (the cable cut, the router shut down — everything behind it stops reporting) and **Tamper / Clean** (a looped camera, a spoofed access point — everything behind it reports false data). Both are shown before they happen. Players see nothing of it: you narrate who comes down to check, and when.
- **Networks · Trace** (right column, on maps with a network) — hackers' pings (decision 170). A ping rolled on a sheet comes here; the console lists what hangs on each network within 10 squares of their marker and sends it back to the sheet — types only, never positions (a node's *Network name*, set in the Maps tab, shows as it is). Every network it touched gains Trace: 1 for a success, 2 for a failure, plus the network's security (set on its uplink: Civilian +0, Corporate +1, Military / AI-run +2). Calm 0–2 · Twitchy 3–4 (a technician checks the logs; cameras sweep) · Alert 5–6 (security is told; a patrol heads for where it was heard — the panel says which node heard it) · Lockdown 7+ (keys change: *Re-key* wipes the players' list of that network; remote hacks off; electric doors fail locked). − / + trims Trace by hand. *Hold ping results for my OK* keeps each result here until you *Send* or *Discard* it. A player's toy drone (6-4) sends you one line per flight — recon or decoy, and how it went: tell them what it saw — and, on this map, adds +1 Trace to the network nearest their marker.
- **Skyline** — on a city map, *Skyline* shows your screen the city at an angle (*Top-down* goes back); tap a district to select it. It changes only your screen: each player chooses on their own sheet.
- **Take the map off the table** when the scene ends.

> **Keep it fun.** If the map and the story disagree, the story wins: move the marker, open the door, reveal the room. If a player struggles with dragging, move their marker for them. The map is there to end arguments, not to start them.

## 9-9. Missions, the session log and factions {#sec-9-9}

**How it works.** The **Story tab** is the campaign's notebook, saved on your computer (`local/gm/campaign.json`) and never published.

- **Missions** — title, code, **objectives** (primary and secondary, marked done or failed), **chapters** for the scenes (*Next chapter ▶*), the zones involved.
- **Notable NPCs** — shared across missions, with their state.
- **Threats** — prepared enemies; *→ Combat* sends them to the tracker.
- **Maps** — link the mission's saved maps; *Edit* opens one in the Maps tab, *On the table* puts it on the Deploy tab.
- **Session log** — 15 lines a page; anything you write, every *🎲 Roll / note*, and every line you pin with 📌 from **At the table** (the live table log).
- **Handouts** — the **🖼 Handout** button in the console header, on any tab, opens images from `local/gm/handouts/`: pick one, tick who gets it (or *Everyone*), add a caption. It arrives on their sheets like a message.
- **AI prompts** — *Build prompt* writes a debrief of the mission (from the log and objectives) or a draft for the next one, to paste into your own AI. *Save to campaign story* keeps the debrief in the campaign story.

**Factions.** The rule: each power has a standing from **−3 Hostile** to **+3 Patron** (−2 Watched, −1 Wary, 0 Neutral, +1 Friendly, +2 Allied). It moves by 1, always with a reason, inside a floor and a ceiling you set ("they'll never trust a crew that hit their depot"). In play it decides who answers a call, what a contact will risk, who shows up uninvited. In the console it sits under the campaign; changes go into the session log, and the AI prompts carry the standings. Players never see it.

## 9-10. Crew and Unity {#sec-9-10}

**The rule.** The crew has one shared **Crew track** from 0 to 10 (start 5). It measures **whether they act together, never whether the act was good** (6-6). +1 when the crew commits to a costly or risky call together, or when you see them cover for each other; −1 when one player overrides a crew that objected. Bands: Broken 0, Fractured 1–3, Steady 4–6, Tight 7–10.

**Unity Checks.** When one character wants to do something the crew may not back — kill the witness, take the side deal — talk it through, then open a check. Everyone but the actor votes in secret on their sheet: *Back*, *Object*, *Stay out*. You see who voted what; the table sees only the tally. The actor always decides; the track records it.

**Drives.** Each character has a Drive — Justice or Ambition — and a line in their own words. It's a compass, never a score. Acting on it at a real cost is a good reason for Spotlight XP.

**In the console — Table tab, Crew & Unity.** The track with *+1 intent* and *−1*; *Open Unity Check* (actor and intent), *Reveal the tally*, then *Backed together +1*, *Override −1* or *No change · close*. Voting by voice works too: just record the outcome.

## 9-11. Experience and levels {#sec-9-11}

**The rule.** No XP per kill. Per mission, each player present gets:

- **3** for a primary objective achieved, **1** for a secondary, **1** for an objective failed (you learn from it);
- **+1** for surviving a fight rated Hard, **+2** for Deadly (the rating when the fight started);
- **+1 Spotlight** from you, at most once or twice per player per session: a great scene, a real cost paid for their Drive.

**10 XP is a level**: a talent point every level, +1 to an attribute on even levels, an extra ability slot at 3, 6 and 9 (5-9). Players spend levels only when you open level-up — usually in downtime, somewhere quiet.

**In the console.**

- **Mission XP** (Story tab) — totals per player from objectives, danger and spotlight; *Award* sends them once per mission.
- **Open level-up for everyone** / *Close level-up* (Table tab), or per player on their card.
- **Gift** a talent from the player's card: free, no prerequisites. Use it for story rewards — training with a veteran, a debt repaid in kind.
- **Washer deal** (player's card): offer to wash cash into Credit Score or cash it out — direction, cash per point, commission %, at most how many points, and a line from the fixer. It waits on their sheet (Gear) until they take it or walk away; the log explains the change to your audit (8-1).

## 9-12. Downtime and debt {#sec-9-12}

**The rule.** Between missions, somewhere quiet, time passes (5-10). Injuries heal and armor is repaired. Lifestyle upkeep is **shown, not charged** — the table settles it. Each character's **debt pressure** rises by 1 (0–3): Squeeze, Escalation, then **Crisis**, which is a mission hook. An instalment or a story resolution lowers it.

**How it plays.** Each character gets one **story event** — a creditor's visit, an old contact, a quiet moment — and the crew may get one together. Events are narrative: their options are shown as choices, never applied by the app. You and the player decide what happens.

**In the console — Story tab → Downtime.** Choose the duration. For each player the console suggests events with a **confidence %** and the reasons (their debt and its stage, career, youth, current state, the world); recently used ones fade. Pick, edit, swap, write your own or skip; add a crew event; open level-up if you like; send. Each sheet restores itself, raises the pressure, adds the event to *Story so far* and shows a report. Set pressure by hand from the player's card when the story pays a debt down.

## 9-13. Loot, the Forge and backstories {#sec-9-13}

**Street prices.** Gear bought officially costs Credit Score (traced); on the street it costs cash at a markup of **×3 to ×10** (untraced). The player picks the markup in the purchase window — you tell them which: ×3 from a friendly fence, ×10 for a rifle the night before a job. The rarest gear is street only.

**The Forge (Admin tab).** For the one-off find: pick a kind and a base item, change its numbers, add bonuses while it's in use (any attribute or skill, armor, actions, OFF, DEF, initiative), a one-line note, a value or *can't be fenced*, and *Forge & send* it to the player who found it. It shows in purple on their sheet and keeps its stats if given away. Forged items are kept for reuse (*Forged so far*).

**Crew stashes (Admin tab, above the Forge).** Set up a stash the crew shares (the van, a lock-up). Players put in and take out from their Gear tab; every move goes in its history, which everyone sees. *Lock* when it matters (watched, impounded, across town): players see it but can't touch it. *Raid* empties it — the history says what was lost. *Remove* hides it; it stays on disk. Their own private stashes show on their player card (📦).

**Backstories (Admin tab).** Players can ask you to write or polish their backstory from the builder or the Dossier. Requests wait here; add a note per request (hooks, ties to the campaign). In Claude Code, `/backstories` writes the waiting ones; you read, edit and send. Nothing reaches a player until you send it.

> **Keep it fun.** Loot is story. A unique item works best with a name, a past and a note — "the grip is wrapped in a dead courier's scarf" — and a small bonus. A +5 is a solved problem; a +1 with a story is a conversation piece.

## 9-14. Mistakes, undo and the archive {#sec-9-14}

**The rule.** Players never undo their own sheets — they ask, and you decide.

**In the console.**

- **↶** (Undo) on a player's card — *Last action*, *This round*, *Whole combat*, or back to before any line of their log. The sheet returns to the snapshot taken just before; rolls stay in the log. Every undo is listed in the **Undo log** (Table tab), with a count on each card, so the table can see it was fair.
- **↶ Undo** on the combat tracker for your own changes, announced to the table (9-6).
- **Admin tab** — characters, missions and session logs on disk. *Archive* moves a finished character or mission out of the way; *Restore* brings it back; *Inspect* reads it. Nothing is ever deleted, and every action asks first.

> **Keep it fun.** Undo is for mistakes — a wrong button, a misread rule, damage applied twice, a fight started too early. It is also for the rare moment when the dice produce something nobody at the table wants: a character dying to a stray bullet in a scene that didn't matter. Use that one sparingly and openly. Tell the table you're doing it and why; then it's a gift, not a cheat.

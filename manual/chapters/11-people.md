# People of 2045 {#ch-11}

## 11. Not everyone is a target

Most of the people a crew meets will never roll initiative. They are fixers and clerks, preachers and patrol officers, idealists with a chalkboard and zealots with a quota. They want something, they know something, and they answer to someone. That makes them more useful than any enemy: a guard who likes you opens doors that no lockpick does.

This chapter is a **collection** of such people from the GM's library, grouped by the movement or company behind them (chapters 3 and 4 describe them all). As with enemies, browse it for the feel of the street. The GM changes, reuses and invents people all the time, and some of these will turn up under another name.

## 11-1. Reading a card {#sec-11-1}

- **Faction and employer** — who they answer to. The two can differ: an Optimizer works *for* the Avalon-Collins Ledger, a Watch officer *is* Centurion-Vanguard.
- **Disposition** — how they feel about people like the crew when they first meet: *Friendly*, *Neutral*, *Suspicious*, *Hostile*. It's where they start, not where they end.
- **Goal** — what they want right now. The most useful line on the card: help them get it, or threaten it, and they will talk.
- **Traits** — how to play them, in three words.

People have **no stat block** and no special abilities in the rules. Their power is what they know, who they know and what they can sign. That is the leverage a crew is after.

## 11-2. People in play {#sec-11-2}

**Talking.** A conversation is roleplay first (6-3); a check comes only when the outcome is uncertain and matters. The player rolls the social skill that fits what they are doing (persuading, lying, intimidating, reading the room); the person doesn't roll. Their disposition and goal set the GM's difficulty step. A good starting point: a Friendly contact asked for something in their interest is *Favourable* or easier; a Suspicious one asked for a risk is *Challenging*; a Hostile one asked to betray their own is *Severe* or simply impossible without leverage. A bribe, a favour or a threat that touches their goal moves the step (6-5).

**Factions.** How a person treats the crew also depends on how their faction sees the crew: the GM's faction tracker (9-9). A Friendly clerk from a faction that has the crew on its watch list is still Friendly, but nervous, and quick to cover herself.

**When a person fights.** Most don't: they run, hide, call the Watch, or put their hands up. If one does, the GM uses the closest card from chapter 10 under their name: a Watch officer fights like a *Watch Patrolman*, a Glitch-Front runner like a *Glitch Anarchist*. Location attacks then work as on any organic or cybernetic target (10-3).

**On the map.** People on the table map are anonymous at first: a role and a number (*Worker 1*, *Security guard 2*), drawn in purple. When the crew learns a name, the GM renames the marker. Each one carries a private note only the GM sees (9-8).

**In the GM console.**

- **Story tab → Notable NPCs** — add people from the library (or a new name) to a mission. Each keeps a **status** (*active*, *captured*, *dead*), a **relationship** to the crew (*allied*, *neutral*, *hostile*) and a GM note, shared across every mission of the campaign.
- **Deploy tab → NPCs** — pick a role and *+ Add* to put a person on the map; rename to reveal who they are.
- **Mission drafts** — the *Draft with AI* prompt can include library people, so new jobs grow from faces the table already knows.

> **Keep it fun.** Give each person one thing the players will remember — a tic, a phrase, a smell — and one thing they want. Recurring people are the campaign's memory: the clerk the crew bribed in the first job should be there, promoted and nervous, in the fifth.

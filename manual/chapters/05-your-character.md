## 5-1. Who You Are & How We Play {#sec-5-1}

Welcome to The Fractured Century. Before you load your character sheet or roll your first die, it is vital to understand who you play, the world you inhabit, and how we build this story together at the table.

### Who You Are

In this universe, you are not a superhero, a chosen one, or a high-tech demigod. You play an **Operator** — a survivor living on the frayed edges of a corporate-dominated wasteland. You are somebody who fell through the cracks of the Credit Score system, carrying real debts, personal trauma, and a burning drive to keep your head above water. You move through the unmonitored Grey Zones where corporate cameras fail, trading raw scrap, copper wire, and physical favors just to buy another day of clean air and drinking water. You survive because you are sharp, adaptable, and willing to take the jobs no official channel will touch.

### What to Expect from the World

This is a grounded, atmospheric setting driven by scarcity, bureaucracy, and quiet desperation. Technology here is not a ladder to ascension; it is the cage constructed to keep populations compliant. Expect unskippable AR ad feeds, degraded bandwidth, cold industrial rain, and an ambient electromagnetic decay that rattles your teeth. Success in this world is rarely about wiping out an army — it is about securing a safe haven, paying off a soul-crushing debt anchor, and living to see tomorrow with your humanity intact.

### Your Role at the Table

- **Immerse & Roleplay:** Step into your character's boots. React to the world through their eyes, their flaws, and their desperate motivations — not just as numbers on a sheet.
- **Contribute to the Narrative:** The Game Master (GM) provides the world and the conflicts, but you drive the story. Your choices, mistakes, and tactical ingenuity shape the outcome of every mission.
- **Dialogue with the GM:** Treat the GM as your narrator and world-engine, not an opponent. Ask questions about your surroundings, describe your intent clearly, and seek creative solutions to brutal problems.
- **Embrace Failure:** In a bleak, high-stakes system, failures and unexpected twists make the story rich. A bad roll is not a dead end — it is a sudden, dramatic shift in the narrative.
- **Cooperate to Survive:** The grid is hostile enough on its own. Your squad is your only true lifeline. Trust your team, play to your strengths, and keep each other alive.

> **Unwritten rule:** the goal of the game is to immerse in a universe and have fun playing. Rules can be bent, rewards can be given, results can be awarded outside cold tables and dice rolls. Roleplaying and contribution can lead to unexpected results.

> **Second unwritten rule:** the GM is there to facilitate and guide you through the story. There are reasons behind the decisions — but the GM is also a human being. They can be wrong. If something feels unfair, say it.

## 5-2. Using the App {#sec-5-2}

The app is a smart paper sheet: it does the maths, keeps the rules one tap away and never asks you to flip through tables. It is not the game — keep your head up and talk to your squad.

- **Builder** — creates a character in about five minutes. Every step explains itself; rolls print in a terminal log; choices you need to make appear inline. An *Advanced mode* lets experienced players type values directly or pick specific events (marked `[MANUAL]` for the GM).
- **Character sheet** — your live sheet during play: HP, body, weapons, class engine, skills, gear, notes. Everything recalculates the moment something changes.
- **Real dice welcome** — every roll window takes the app's roll or yours. After a hit, the window goes step by step: damage, then (Scavenger) Exploit, then the hit location of a location attack. At each step press *Roll*, or type what your dice show and press *Enter my dice*; the summary comes at the end.
- **Guided tours** — the **?** button at the top of the sheet walks you through it in about two minutes: status, weapons, your turn, the roll window, your class engine, checks, the market and the Dossier. The engine card has a **Class tour** for your class's resource and abilities, and the Map tab has a **Map tour** (7-2 §5). Each is offered once; the **?** buttons replay them.
- **Manual** — this book. Any ⓘ on the sheet or in the builder opens a short explanation with a link to the full rule here.
- **Language** — the EN / IT switch at the top changes the app and this manual. Game terms stay in English, so you and the GM use the same words.

### Saving your character

- Characters are **saved automatically** in the browser you use. Use the same device and browser each session.
- **Export** downloads a backup file. Do it after each session, and whenever the GM asks for your current sheet.
- **Import** loads a backup on any device. Files from the old v0.8 pages (`char_init_*.json`, `Save_*.JSON`) are converted automatically.

### Playing at the table

The GM can run the app from a laptop on the table's Wi-Fi: every tablet opens the same address and works even without internet. At the table the sheet plays a soft sound when your combat turn starts, when the GM sends you something and when you are hit; the 🔔 button in the sheet header switches it off on that device. (Browsers only allow sound after your first tap on the page.) Opening the files directly by double-click does not work — the app must be served from a computer (ask your GM).

**Play as… — your characters on any device.** At the table, the characters page starts with **At the table · Play as**: pick your name (or *New player…* the first time). The list then shows only your characters (other players' on the same tablet fold behind a switch). Your characters at the table appear there, even ones made on another tablet — *Open here* copies one onto this device. Opening a character joins the table with it; characters you create or import while playing as yourself are saved to the table too, under your name. If two devices have played the same character, the newest save wins (the table keeps every earlier copy, so the GM can always go back); if it is open on another device right now, you're asked first. Without a table nothing changes: your sheets stay in this browser, and Export / Import work as always.

## 5-3. Attributes & Checks {#sec-5-3}

Your Operator's physical, mental, and social capabilities are defined by eight Core Attributes. These drive every roll, skill check, and combat action you perform on the grid.

::table[attributes]

### Bonuses and skill checks {#sec-5-3-skills}

You rarely roll against raw attributes. Every attribute has a **bonus equal to half its value, rounded down**. Every skill combines the bonuses of two attributes, and that sum is the skill's **Target Number (TN)**.

**Roll a d20: if the result is equal to or lower than the TN, you succeed.** The GM may make it harder by applying a difficulty penalty (see *Skill Checks & Difficulty*, 6-2).

> **Example:** Thrown Objects uses DEX + INT. DEX 10 (bonus +5) and INT 12 (bonus +6) give TN 11: roll 11 or less on a d20.

Because every attribute feeds several skills, neglecting one leaves blind spots across the whole tree. The sheet calculates all skills live — tap any skill to roll it.

**The roll window.** Tapping a skill or an attribute opens it. From top to bottom:
- **TN, Difficulty and Roll ≤** — your TN, the difficulty step (the GM's table difficulty is picked for you; red when harder, green when easier, 6-2) and the number you need.
- **In play** — items and effects already counted in your TN: a stim you took, a drug, an optic. Nothing to do; it tells you why the number is what it is.
- **Gear that helps** — tools you carry that fit this skill (a lockpick set, a medkit). Tick the one that fits; only one counts, and the GM can say it doesn't apply here.
- **Light** — only in the dark (7-2 §4): the sheet pre-picks the line from the map or the GM's Light switch; change it if the scene says otherwise.
- **Roll** — press *Roll d20*, or type what your own die shows and press *Resolve*. Margins, near misses and natural 1 / 20 are spelled out.

> **Locks: three ways through a door.** A **mechanical** lock (a padlock, an old tenement door) is **Sleight of Hand** — the Mechanical Lockpick Set adds +2. An **electronic** lock or card reader is **Electronics / Security** — the Keycard Bypass Cloner adds +3. A lock **wired to a network** (a corporate door on the site AI) is **Computers / Hacking**, from a terminal or a network you have pinged. Or skip finesse: forcing a door, crate or panel is a **STR** check, and the Crowbar & Mini-Pry Set adds +3.

::table[skills]

### Derived combat values {#sec-5-3-combat}

Six tactical values come from the **raw sum of two attributes**, looked up in the table below. Some life events shift you one row up or down for a specific value (for example *Long Legs* moves your movement one row down the table, which is better).

::table[combat]

- **Initiative** = half DEX + half WIS (rounded down each) − 10. Roll d20 + Initiative at the start of combat.
- **Max HP** = CON + MST + 10.

### Health, armor and injuries {#sec-5-3-body}

You have **one pool of HP** (CON + MST + 10) and **one armor value**: the sum of the armor you wear, plus cover in a fight. Every hit subtracts your armor from the damage; what is left comes off your HP.

Your body matters when someone makes a **location attack** (7-5): they take −4 TN and roll where it lands. Damage minus armor comes off HP as usual, and then:

- **If any damage gets through your armor**, the location adds a condition: leg → *Crippled leg*, arm → *Broken arm*, abdomen → *Prone*, chest → *Slowed*, head → *Stunned* (*Incapacitated* if the hit leaves you at half HP or less).
- **If your armor stops it completely**, your armor **cracks by 1** until it is repaired.

Normal attacks never crack armor. Broken arms and crippled legs stay until treated — First aid, a Trauma Patch, or downtime — and count left and right separately. The sheet's **Health & conditions** card does the maths: enter the damage under *Take a hit*, and say where if it was a location attack.

### Building your Operator {#sec-5-3-buy}

At creation every attribute starts at **6** and you have **20 points** to spend. No attribute may go above **14** at this stage (life events and careers can push it higher later). Credit Score starts at **20**: you can lower it to gain points, but you can never raise it above 20 during creation.

- **Avoid min-maxing.** A 6 CON operator collapses under minimal damage; a 6 WIS operator is constantly blindsided. Balanced builds survive longer.
- **Not sure?** Take the playstyle quiz in the builder. It suggests a class and a ready-made attribute spread.

## 5-4. Youth: Adolescence Events {#sec-5-4}

Before your Operator walked the streets as an adult, their early life was shaped by the grid. **Three events, each rolled on 2d20**, define what happened to you up to age 15. Middle results are common; the extremes (2 and 40) are rare.

Events can change attributes, shift a combat value one row, change your maximum HP, give or clear a criminal record, grant an education credential or waive future career requirements. When an event asks for a choice (for example *+2 to INT or WIS*), the builder asks you directly.

> Results that ruin your concept? Ask the GM — they can authorise a re-roll.

::table[adolescence]

## 5-5. Career & Debt {#sec-5-5}

After 15 you can go straight to the streets, or serve one or more **career terms** first. Careers are optional.

- **Entry roll:** each term, roll **d20 ≤ your higher of INT and CHA, +4**. Every term you have already served in a row in the same career adds **+1** (the boss knows you). Criminal and Wage Slave take anyone, no roll; some life events waive it. **Fail** and you spend those two years **unemployed** (Drifter / Outcast), the only way into that row.
- **Each term:** +2 years of age, the career's attribute and Credit Score changes, **two career events** (2d20), then an aging check. A career makes you better at one thing and costs you another: no career gives both stats of a class.
- **Broke:** if a career pushes your Credit Score below 0 you are **wanted for non-payment**: a criminal record, and your career phase ends on the spot.
- **Life-path cap:** no attribute goes above **18** from events and careers.
- **Requirements:** minimum attributes, maximum age, education credentials, a clean record or a minimum Credit Score. The builder marks each one ✓ or ✗. Some life events waive requirements.
- **Push your luck:** every extra term is more experience, more risk, and more age. Some events end your career phase on the spot.

## 5-6. Classes {#sec-5-6}

Classes represent your Operator's operational doctrine: how raw attributes become tactical action. Each class is a distinct **engine** with its own resource loop and risk/reward rhythm. Your **subclass** sets your two main attributes — their average is your **Class TN** — and which abilities you can equip.

::table[classes]

### Equipping abilities (brain fog) {#sec-5-6-equip}

You know every ability of your subclass, but combat stress limits how many you can keep at hand: you **equip up to 3** (the GM may raise the limit as you gain experience). Swap freely between combats. An equipped ability is **ready** only when your engine allows it — the sheet shows ready abilities in green and tells you what is missing for the others.

### The Soldier {#sec-5-6-soldier}

The Soldier is the team's front-line powerhouse, built on **Stance-Flow**: momentum is banked as **Tactical Points (TP)** and spent on finishers that move you between stances.

- **Ready:** where every fight starts — normal damage dealt and taken, no bonus.
- **Defensive:** ×0.5 damage dealt and taken. Hold ground or recover.
- **Attack:** normal damage, and **+2 TN on weapon attacks**.
- **Onslaught:** ×1.5 damage dealt and taken. High risk, high reward.

**The flow.** Every fight starts in **Ready**. Only stance abilities move you to another stance. A **normal attack keeps your stance** (that is what a stance is for: shots at ×1.5 in Onslaught, ×0.5 when dug in), and so does *Aim* before one. **Anything else drops you back to Ready** — moving, reloading, swapping weapons, interacting, using an item or a talent — but your **TP stay**. A stance lasts through the enemies' turns and into your next turn until one of those actions: end a turn in Onslaught and every hit you take before then is ×1.5.

**TP** start at **0 every combat** and have no cap. You build them by fighting: **every normal attack that hits gives +1 TP**, *Brace & Dig In* gives +2 and *Steady Aim* +1. Finishers cost 1–3 TP and shift your stance. The sheet applies your stance to every damage roll.

- **Grunt (STR / CON):** builds in Defensive, pushes into Attack, then charges into Onslaught and stays there — *Fix Bayonets*, *Bunker Buster*, *Reckless Execution*.
- **Officer (DEX / INT):** lives between Defensive and Attack and turns TP into the team's actions and accuracy — *Commanding Directive*, *Coordinated Crossfire*, *Tactical Redeployment*.

::table[abilities:soldier]

### The Hacker {#sec-5-6-hacker}

The Hacker fights through information warfare, balancing **RAM** against **Heat**. Both limits come from the **deck** you carry — better decks and hardware mods mean more RAM and a higher heat cap.

- **Instant hacks** need free RAM while they run and add heat.
- **Sustained hacks** keep their RAM locked until you release them: tap ✕ on one in the deck panel, or use *Release Payload* to end them all.
- **Heat** builds with every hack and drops by 1 at the end of each round. If it reaches the deck's cap, the deck **Blue Screens**: every sustained hack crashes (their RAM is freed), the deck is locked for the next round, then it reboots at cap − 3. *Thermal Dump* vents 3 heat; *System Hard Reboot* clears everything but keeps the deck offline for the rest of this round and the next.
- **Range:** a device in sight, or within 10 squares on a network you have pinged.
- **What you can touch:** enemies are **organic**, **cybernetic** or **synthetic**. Most hacks only work on cybernetic or synthetic targets; organic ones are reached through the gear around them (doors, turrets, comms). The GM tells you which is which.
- *Thermal Dump*, *Release Payload* and *System Hard Reboot* need no roll; they still take an action.

- **Scammer (INT / CHA):** social engineering, credential spoofing, distraction and cheap sustained debuffs.
- **Master (INT / WIS):** heavy system destruction, network control and high-heat anti-tech payloads.

::table[abilities:hacker]

### The Tech Wizard {#sec-5-6-techwiz}

The Tech Wizard is a battlefield architect and field medic who tunes a rig to the infrastructure around them — power lines, scrap, wiring. Their engine is **Field Sync**: the longer they hold one purpose, the deeper the sync.

- **Two modes.** **Mend** (support: mend cracked armor, heal, raise cover, buff) or **Surge** (offense: crack armor, shock, traps, turrets). Every ability has a Mend face and a Surge face; your mode picks which one fires.
- **Sync 0–3:** *Cold → Warm → Tuned → Resonant*. +1 each time you use an ability, and +1 at the end of a round in which you did not switch mode.
- **Switching** mode is free at the start of your turn, but Sync drops to 0.
- **Thresholds:** stronger abilities need a minimum Sync.
- **Resonant cast:** at Sync 3 you may fire an ability with **both faces at once** — the cover shelters your friend *and* the shrapnel hits the enemy. Sync then drops to 0 (you keep your mode).
- **Brownout:** the heaviest moves (*Structural Collapse*) drop Sync to 0 and take the rig offline — no abilities — until the end of the next round.
- **Disruption:** losing HP costs 1 Sync. Keep your Tech Wizard behind cover.
- Every combat starts at Cold.

- **Mechanic (CON / WIS):** leans Mend — fortifications, armor repair, healing.
- **Engineer (DEX / WIS):** leans Surge — gadgets that keep working: the shock conduit and the sentry turret act at round end without spending Sync.

::table[abilities:techwiz]

### The Smuggler {#sec-5-6-smuggler}

The Smuggler is a high-speed opportunist riding the **Adrenaline Pendulum**, a spectrum from **Cold Calculation (−4)** through **Composure (0)** to **Adrenaline Rush (+4)**. Every ability **needs** a side of the pendulum and **shifts** it when used.

- **Cold (−4 to −1):** steady hands — **+2 TN on gun attacks**. Unlocks the planning moves: *Contraband Stash*, *Safehouse Route*, *Decoy Deployment*, *Black-Market Ammo*, *Call in Favor*.
- **Composure (0):** no bonus; only the abilities that need nothing (*Street Snap-Shot*, *Bribe / Distraction*).
- **Hot (+1 to +4):** blood up — **+1 damage on every damage roll and +1 square per move**. Unlocks the reckless moves: *Pistol Whip & Rob*, *Adrenaline Rush*, *Desperate Gamble*.
- **Elasticity:** most Cold moves heat you up, most Hot moves cool you down. *Desperate Gamble* goes all-in and crashes you to −3.
- **Pain:** a wounded Smuggler drifts +1 toward Adrenaline at the end of each round.

The sheet applies the zone bonuses for you: the Attack TN of your guns, your damage rolls and the squares on the Move button.

- **Dealer (DEX / CHA):** agile gunman and trickster — quick-draw strikes, black-market ammo, close-range robbery.
- **Trafficker (WIS / CHA):** tactical mastermind and off-grid navigator — map control, decoys, underground favors.

::table[abilities:smuggler]

### The Scavenger {#sec-5-6-scavenger}

The Scavenger reads the fight before joining it. It picks the dangerous target, learns how it moves, then cashes that knowledge in all at once, or takes what the enemy carries. No career is required: anyone can live this way at night, though Drifters fit it best. Its engine is **Tells**, and Tells live **on the enemies**, not in a pool.

- **Gaining Tells:** at the start of each of your turns, place **1 Tell** on an enemy you can see within your optic's range. Place **2** if you are **Unseen**: in **full cover** and you did not attack last round. Being Unseen only speeds up your Tells; enemies can still shoot at you.
- **Limits:** at most **3 Tells on one enemy**, and only as many enemies hold your Tells as your **optic can Track**.
- **Losing Tells:** an enemy's Tells are gone when it goes down, or at the end of a round in which you lost sight of it.
- **Your target:** tap an enemy on the sheet to make it your target. Abilities spend **that enemy's** Tells.
- **Exploit (always on):** when an attack hits, the roll window asks which enemy you hit and how many of its Tells to spend; each adds **+1d4 damage** (rolled or typed) and is used up. A miss spends nothing.
- **Salvage (always on):** after a fight, take one extra item from what the enemies dropped (Liftable list below).

Build Tells on the enemy that really matters: a full burst is +3d4 on one hit, but it takes about two rounds, so it is wasted on a cheap drone.

At the live table your Tells sit on the GM's **Hostiles** list (by letter), and the GM sees them. Without a GM-run fight, name the enemies yourself on the sheet ("Heavy", "Drone 2").

**What Lift can take (the GM decides, using this list):**

- **Yes:** a magazine; consumables (stims, medkits, grenades); small tools; keycards, data chips and creds; civilian or junk weapons (knives, junk pistols).
- **AI-locked:** military and corporate weapons are biometric-locked: in unauthorised hands they brick. *Strip* can disarm them, never make them yours.
- **Never:** worn armor, cyberware, or anything on a synthetic or drone.

**Optics.** Everyone wears basic AR lenses (range 4, Track 1). A Scavenger's optic is the professional version, bought on the market like a deck: **Range** (squares within which you place Tells), **Track** (how many enemies can hold your Tells at once) and one **mode**: *Thermal* (Tells through smoke, darkness and partial cover), *Motion* (once per turn, +1 Tell on an enemy that moved since your last turn), *Long-glass* (range doubled while in full cover), *Pattern* (Tells are lost only when the target goes down).

::table[optics]

- **Prowler (DEX / CON):** close and quick-handed: reads an enemy face to face (*Close Study*), lifts what it carries, jams its gun and strips its plates.
- **Stalker (CON / INT):** patient eye at a distance: *Case the Room*, marks targets for the crew, *Called Shot* and *Kill Shot*.

::table[abilities:scavenger]

## 5-7. Story & Portrait {#sec-5-7}

The builder's last steps turn your rolls into a story. It compiles two prompts from everything you rolled:

- **Backstory prompt** — paste it into any AI assistant (or use it as a writing guide) and paste the result back. It already contains the setting, your events, career and debt. With the app in Italian, it asks the AI to write the story in Italian.
- **Or ask the GM** — at the live table, *Send to the GM* (in the builder) or *Ask the GM to write it* (Dossier) hands the same prompt to the GM, who has it written, reads it and sends it to your sheet. It lands on the Dossier; you can still edit it, or go back to your previous version. With a backstory already written, the button asks the GM to polish it instead.
- **Portrait prompt** — for an image generator (both prompts stay on the Dossier, under *Prompts for your own AI*).
- **Appearance & manner** — gender, build, hair, eyes, marks and attitude, in your own words. They flow into both prompts (whatever you leave blank, the portrait prompt asks you to add). Pure flavour: no rule reads them and the GM's change check ignores them, so edit them on the Dossier whenever you like.

Upload the portrait on the sheet (Dossier tab); it is shrunk automatically so saves stay small. *Frame the photo* (under it) zooms, moves and brightens it — or drag the photo — and the same framing shows everywhere, the GM's screen too.

## 5-8. The Character Sheet {#sec-5-8}

- **Status bar** — always visible: HP, armor, initiative, actions, Class TN, Credit Score, cash and active conditions.
- **Play** — two rows in the order of a turn: the Turn panel (*Start combat*, *End round*, one *Attack* for the weapon in hand, *?* shows what each action does) beside your weapons (attack TN, damage, ammo, reload); then your class engine, equipped abilities and talents beside health, armor, injuries and conditions (with *Take a hit*) and cover. Move, OFF, DEF and PER sit on the header's second line, changed live by conditions.
- **Skills** — attributes (adjustable for injuries, aging or GM rulings) and every skill. Tap to roll with the GM's difficulty.
- **Gear** — cash, inventory, the market, and your lifestyle.
- **Dossier** — identity, class, background, debt (with its pressure and instalments), portrait, backstory and *Story so far* (5-10).
- **Notes & maps** — your field notes, **Maps we've seen** (every map that was on the table, as far as your crew saw it, 7-2 §5) and a log of rolls, purchases and ability uses.
- **Progress** — level, XP, and the talent trees (5-9).
- **Active map** — only while the GM has a map on the table: look, move, open doors, check line of sight and range (7-2 §5).


## 5-9. Advancement & Talents {#sec-5-9}

Your Operator grows by surviving missions, not by counting bodies. There is **no XP per kill**: what earns XP is what the crew achieves and the risks it takes.

### Experience (XP) {#sec-5-9-xp}

At the end of a mission the GM awards XP to everyone who took part:

- **Main objective succeeded:** 3 XP · **secondary objective succeeded:** 1 XP · **objective failed:** 1 XP (you learn from it).
- **A dangerous fight survived:** +1 if it was *Hard*, +2 if *Deadly* — rated by the GM's challenge meter when the fight started. Avoiding a fight costs you nothing.
- **Spotlight:** +1 (at most 2 per mission) for a clever plan, great roleplay, or pushing your debt's story.

A mission is usually worth 5–8 XP.

### Levels {#sec-5-9-levels}

**10 XP = 1 level** (up to level 10) — roughly a level every one and a half to two missions. Each level gives:

- **Every level:** 1 **talent point**.
- **Levels 2, 4, 6, 8, 10:** +1 to one attribute (the usual caps and aging still apply).
- **Levels 3, 6, 9:** +1 equipped ability slot (on top of your brain-fog limit).
- **Level 5:** tier-3 talents unlock.

XP arrives at once, but you **spend** a level only when the GM opens **level-up** — downtime, somewhere quiet between missions. A heist followed by an escape stays one long run. Rare extra **training** (time and money in downtime) can raise an attribute too, if the GM allows it.

### Talents {#sec-5-9-talents}

Talents are mostly **passive edges**. Each costs **1 talent point**.

- **Trees:** three general trees for everyone (*Survival*, *Street*, *Tactics*) and one tree for your class, with branches for each subclass.
- **Tiers:** tier 1 any time; **tier 2** from level 3, after a tier-1 talent of the same tree; **tier 3** from level 5, after a tier-2 talent of the same tree. At most **two tier-3 talents**.
- Some need a minimum attribute (e.g. *Iron Jaw* needs CON 12).
- **Automatic** talents are applied by the sheet (HP, initiative, armor, class engines…). **At the table** talents are short rules you and the GM apply; once-per-round / combat / mission talents get a *Use* button on the Play tab.
- The GM may also **gift** a talent as a story reward — free, and without prerequisites.

The Progress tab shows every tree, what you can take now, and why the rest is locked.

## 5-10. Downtime {#sec-5-10}

Between missions, when the crew is somewhere quiet, the GM can call a **downtime**: a few days, weeks or months pass. Back-to-back missions (a heist, then the escape) are one long run — downtime waits for a safe moment.

When downtime ends, each player gets a **report**:

- **Rest:** HP and armor come back. Broken arms and crippled legs heal too, if enough time passed.
- **Upkeep:** what your lifestyle costs for the period. It is **shown, not charged** — settle it with the GM: pay, sell something, negotiate.
- **A story event:** something from *your* life — your debt, your old career, your past, or the state you're in. The options are ideas, never automatic: talk it through with the GM and choose.
- **Level-up** usually opens at the same time (5-9).

Every event goes into **Story so far** on your Dossier, so your character's life between jobs builds up over the campaign.

### Debt pressure {#sec-5-10-debt}

Your debt doesn't wait. Its **pressure** goes from 0 to 3 and rises by **1 at every downtime**:

- **1 · Squeeze** — the first warning. An instalment of **$300** lowers it.
- **2 · Escalation** — things get worse. Instalment **$800**.
- **3 · Crisis** — the threat is here, and it usually becomes a mission. Instalment **$2,000**. Once the crisis is dealt with, pressure goes back to 1.

Paying is **your choice**: the *Pay instalment* button on the Dossier takes the cash and lowers pressure by one step. The story can lower it too — a deal, a favour, a mission — and a few hard story paths can end a debt for good.

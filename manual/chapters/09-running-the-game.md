# Running the Game {#ch-9}

## 9. Behind the screen

This chapter is for the Game Master. It covers the rules that are yours to apply, how they play out at the table, and where each one lives in the **GM console** (the `/gm/` page the table server opens for you). Each section follows the same order: **the rule**, **how it plays**, **in the console**. Where the two are the same thing, they are merged.

The app is a smart paper sheet for everyone, the GM included. It does the maths and remembers what happened; it does not run the game. Every number it shows can be overridden by you, and every tool in the console that changes a sheet can be taken back.

## 9-1. Fun first {#sec-9-1}

**The rule.** The GM's job is a good evening at the table, not a correct simulation. The rules exist to make choices matter and outcomes surprising. When a rule gets in the way of that, you change the ruling and keep playing. Players should never feel the app is the referee: you are.

**How it plays.** Watch the table, not only the screen. Signs a scene isn't working:

- phones out, side conversations, the same player talking for twenty minutes;
- a fight past round four where nothing has changed but the HP numbers;
- three failed rolls on the same door with nothing new learned;
- a player who has been quiet all evening.

Most of the time the problem is the scene, not the dice. The encounter you built may be too long, too flat or too hard. Change it while it runs:

- **End a fight that is decided.** Enemies surrender, flee, call for a truce or simply break. Narrate the mop-up instead of rolling it.
- **Collapse a chain of rolls into one.** A whole lock, a whole climb, a whole search: one check, and the margin says how well it went.
- **Fail forward.** A failure still moves the story: the door opens but the alarm trips, the clue is found but someone saw you (6-3).
- **Move the difficulty.** Easier tiers exist for this (9-4): help, time, good tools.
- **Hand someone the spotlight.** Ask the quiet player what their character notices; the Explore tab tells you what they are good at (9-5).

**Overrides are yours.** Every one of these is in the console, and none of them needs an excuse:

- **Difficulty** for one roll or the whole table (9-4).
- **Your own dice and notes**, outside the rules (*🎲 Roll / note*, 9-4).
- **Undo** a player's action, a round or a whole fight (9-14).
- **Accept or revert** changes to a sheet (9-3).
- **Gift** talents, XP, items and unique gear (9-11, 9-13).
- **Hide, reveal, move** anything on the map (9-8).

> **Keep it fun — what this does not mean.** It does not mean rerolling every failure or saving every character. Failure, cost and loss are what make success feel earned, and players notice when nothing can go wrong. Use overrides to fix *the evening* (a scene that drags, a rule that misfired, a mistake with a button), not to remove risk. A good test: would the table thank you for it if they saw you do it?

## 9-2. Opening the table {#sec-9-2}

**How it works.** The live table runs from one computer on the room's Wi-Fi. `npm run table` starts the server and prints two things: the **address** players open on their tablets and the **GM key**. Open `/gm/` on your laptop and type the key. Nothing needs internet.

Players open the address, pick their name under **At the table · Play as** on the characters page and open a character: that joins the table with it (the Dossier's **Join the table** still works too). Every character they create, import or open while playing as themselves is kept by the table under their name, so it follows them to any tablet; when two devices played the same one, the newest save wins and the history keeps the rest. **Admin → Players** lists every player and their characters: rename, merge two entries for the same person, move a character to the right player, remove an empty name. From then on their sheet sends its state to you after every action, and receives your messages, difficulty, light, combat order and map.

**In the console — Table tab.**

- **Player cards** — one per connected character: HP, armor, conditions and injuries, the class engine, readied weapons, the round's actions, level and XP, cash and CRS, the debt, the last log lines.
- **Message a player** — the field at the bottom of each card. Messages wait in a mailbox until the sheet has shown them, so a tablet that was asleep still gets them.
- **Tell everyone** — the bar at the top; *Refresh* asks every sheet to send its state again.
- **Sit out** — hides a character and keeps it out of fights (a player who joined with the wrong sheet, someone who left early). It goes to **Not playing now**, with offline characters; *Bring back* returns it (also from the Scene tab's *Players* list). It lasts the session: after 12 hours the character plays again by itself.
- **Table log** — every action, roll and purchase as it happens. The 📌 in the Story tab pins lines into the session log (9-9).
- **Whispers** — players write to you privately from their sheet (✉ GM in the sheet's bar, while at the table). They land in the Table log marked *✉ to you* with a toast; *↩ Reply* jumps to that player's message field. Only you see them; they're kept on disk, so a reloaded console still has them.
- **On a player card** — *↺ Refill* (in a fight) gives back the round's actions and clears borrowed ones, for a mistake or a ruling; *+ XP* grants extra XP with a reason that goes in their log. A player can sit at the table with one character at a time: opening a second one asks, and the first leaves with a notice. Renaming an enemy (*Players see*) gives it the initials of its new name on the tracker, the map and the players' cards.

Sheets play a soft sound when a player's turn starts, when you send something and when they are hit. Players switch it off on their own device.

**The console's tabs.** The session ones come first — **Explore**, **Table** and **Scene** (the one screen to keep open while you play: the map, the fight, the players, the rolls — 9-8) — then, after the *Prep* label, the ones you use between sessions: **Story**, **Maps**, **Admin**. (Play and Combat gave way to Scene, decision 232; old links to them open it.)

**Activity (🔔 in the header).** What happened at the table and may need you lands in one list, newest first, with a red count of the new ones: a player's hit waiting for you, a hit applied or dodged, the result of a roll you asked for, a change waiting for your OK, a contact on the map, a whisper, Intel. Toasts still show and fade; the list stays (the last 60, on this computer). *Go* takes you to the right tab and panel; opening the list marks it read, *Clear* empties it. A line about a **lock** carries buttons (decision 256): *🎯 Ask …* opens the roll window with the right skill and that player ticked (*Sleight of Hand* for a handle, *Electronics & Security* for an electric door, *Computers & Hacking* for a remote request), and, when the door is on a network, *+1 Trace on B* — tap it if the attempt should be noticed. A hacker running *Door Control* on a scanned door rolls on their sheet: that line only offers the Trace. **Needs you** (decision 260): a line that waits on you — a change waiting for your OK, a contact, anything with buttons (a hazard hit to send, a lock, a valve, a remote door) — stays **open** until you use its buttons or tick **✓**. Open lines float over the map on the Scene tab (top right, the newest three, *+n more* opens the list), like players' hits at the bottom; the 🔔 counts them in red and the rest unread in grey.

**Keyboard shortcuts** (not while typing): **N** next turn (or end the round), **U** undo the last combat step, **R** ask for a roll, **P** go to Play, **?** the list.

## 9-3. The sheet watch {#sec-9-3}

**The rule.** Players own their sheets, and a browser can't stop anyone editing one. So the console doesn't block changes; it **notices** them.

**How it plays.** Changes from play — HP, ammo, conditions, class resources, notes — always pass. Changes to the **foundation** — attributes, CRS, cash, inventory, credentials, class, XP, talents — are checked against the sheet's own log. A purchase explains the cash it cost; a gift explains the item. What the log doesn't explain is **flagged**.

**In the console.** A flagged card shows **Unexplained changes**: what changed, from what to what. *Accept* makes it the new baseline; *Revert* sends the old value back to the sheet. Every snapshot is kept on disk (`local/table/history/`), so nothing is ever lost.

**Kept by the table.** The baselines — what you accepted for each character — live on the table server (decision 234), so the other PC sees the same flags and a cleared browser accepts nothing by accident. The watch starts the first time a character joins: what it brings then is its starting point. **An old app:** a sheet opened before the table was updated says *reload* by itself; its card shows *old app — reload* until it does.

**Changed by hand.** HP set with the sheet's − / +, a weapon's rounds with its ammo − / +, and conditions, boosts or injuries toggled by hand are play, so they pass — but each leaves a line in the table feed (*By hand: HP 30 → 33*, *By hand: Slowed on*; taps in a row make one line) and waits on the player's card under **✋ Changed by hand**, with a red **●** count on the *Table* tab while any wait (unexplained changes count too). *Accept* is the usual answer; *Revert* takes back only that change (HP moves back by the same amount, the condition goes back as it was), so damage taken since then stays.

**Strain on the cards** (6-7, the logic in [12-12](#/manual/sec-12-12)). Each player card shows Strain, its band and Scars, with **+1** (something hard to take) and **+2** (something horrific); a reason you type goes on their card, and *Table → Strain* gives it to everyone. The rest runs by itself: dropping below half HP, fumbles, a crewmate going down (+2 to the others), debt at its worst at a mission's first scene, a Hum zone. *🎬 New scene* asks whether it's a safe moment (everyone −1). *Needs you* tells you when someone is **Fraying** — its **✉ Whisper to …** sends them alone a private line (what only the frayed notice; whether it's real, the game never says) — and when someone hits a **breaking point**: their sheet rolls it, you narrate.

**A fall** (6-8, decision 272). *⤓ Fall…* on a player's card opens **⚡ Harm** on *A fall*: pick the height (3 / 6 / 9 / 15 m or type it), the dice follow (1d6 per full 3 m above 3 m), roll them or type yours, send. Their sheet offers *Land it* (Agility & Athletics: half the damage) or *Take it*; a failure leaves them Prone, a natural 20 cripples a leg, armor applies. Activity tells you how they landed.

> **Keep it fun.** A flag is almost always a mistake — a typo in an attribute, a test purchase, a player fixing something you told them to fix. Ask first, out loud and kindly. The watch is there so nobody has to wonder, not to catch anyone.

## 9-4. Checks, difficulty and light {#sec-9-4}

**The rule.** Every check is a d20 rolled **under** a Target Number (TN): the skill or attribute TN, changed by one **difficulty step** for the whole situation (6-2). Harder steps lower the TN; the easier ones — **Favourable +2, Easy +4, Very easy +6** — raise it. Help is not a separate rule: one helper is Favourable, two people giving each other a leg-up is Easy, the whole crew with the right gear is Very easy. One step, never stacked, and only the person acting rolls. Missing by 1 can be a success with a cost; a natural 1 or 20 adds something the GM narrates. Contested and passive checks are in 6-2.

**How it plays.** Call for a roll only when failure would be interesting. Opening an unlocked door, buying lunch, knowing what your own career taught you: no roll. When you do call for one, say the step out loud, or set it for the table.

The sheet already counts what the player carries: tools appear as tick boxes (one counts), active items and stims show as *In play*, and in the dark a *Light* row suggests the right line (7-2 §4). You have the last word on all three: "the lockpick doesn't help with a keycard" is your call.

**In the console — Table tab, top row.**

- **Table difficulty** — Off, or a step from *Very easy +6* to the hardest. It appears on every sheet as a banner and is pre-picked in every roll window until you switch it off. For a single roll, just tell the player which step to pick.
- **Light** — *Bright* / *Dark* for checks when there is no map on the table. With a map, each player's own square decides (9-8).
- **🎯 Skill check** (console header) — the *Ask for a roll* form of the Explore tab in a window, on any tab: pick a skill or attribute, tick who rolls, a word on why, *Ask*; the results come back under it.
- **🎲 Roll / note** (console header) — your own dice and notes, outside the rules: any expression (`3d6+2`), an optional TN to roll under, *About* (a player or an NPC) and a note. *Log* writes one line into the live mission's session log. Use it for secret rolls, NPC checks, random tables, or just to remember something said at the table. Nothing reaches the sheets.
- **🧩 Puzzle…** (in *🎲 Roll / note*, decisions 243, 247) — a lock, a panel, a password or a coded message the crew solves at the table. Pick **Wires** (which wire to cut), **Keypad** (the door code), **Password**, **Cipher** (a message some app scrambled, letters and numbers alike, each player getting it in their own language — and **How**: *Shift*, *Mirror* (A for Z), *Phone keys* (old keypad presses), *Keyword* (Vigenère: the key word is found elsewhere — even another puzzle's answer), *Zigzag* (the letters reordered along 2–3 lines), or *Any* by difficulty, which on hard can combine Zigzag and Shift; the old-phone keypad and the keyword grid reach their sheets with the clue that names them; type **your own message** under How — optional Italian, else the English goes to everyone; accents and punctuation drop out — or leave it empty for a random one) or **Three helpers** (the tenant's assistants all remember the code — one is hallucinating), and *easy* / *normal* / *hard* — hard always has an assistant that is confidently wrong. **Build** one (*Another one* for a new one). Every puzzle is in two parts: what is **at the lock** (the worn keys, the panel's fault display, the login screen, the scrambled message) goes out with *Send the puzzle*; the clues **to be found** are listed for you, each with where it is (the access log, a technician's voice memo, the target's feed, a colleague, the company's message filter…) and the roll that earns it — **🎯 Ask** opens *Ask for a roll* with that skill set, on top of the puzzle; *Send clue* when they earn it, even scenes later: it **joins the puzzle on their sheets**. The puzzle stays as you left it — close the window, even reload the console, and it's still there, sent clues marked. The checker makes sure what is at the lock is never enough, and that all the clues together leave exactly one answer. You also get the answer and why, three **wrong tries** to read out (flavour — nothing locks by itself), an optional **pressure** line (start a ⏱ Timer yourself if you like), three **hints** (*Send* one when they're stuck), a **roll instead** for a table not in the mood (*Set up the roll*) and an image prompt. The players answer out loud, at the table; the answer goes into your session log.
  > **“Why don't we just ask our AI?”** You will hear it. In 2045 AI can always *try* — it is never free. **It won't:** consumer assistants run under the Compliance Acts — “help me open a lock that isn't mine” is refused *and reported* (a Credit Score flag); every question goes to a company server and is sold on — maybe to the very fixer whose message you're reading; and it stamps your location at the scene. **It can't:** what runs offline in a burner or your lenses is a cut-down model, fine for shopping lists, hopeless at logic; locks, panels and privacy apps ship with anti-AI noise — decoy codes, screens that read differently to a camera — so a photo gets a confident wrong answer, or counts as an attempt (*ATTEMPT 2 OF 3*); and the places worth guarding are often shielded — no signal at all. **It costs:** an uncensored grey-market model exists — a hacker can run one, for time, money or a favour (that's the puzzle's *roll instead*); heavy AI use near the wrong signals brings on the Hum (an MST roll, if you like); and a chat log saying “decode this” is a confession. **The culture:** everyone has been burned by an AI that was confidently wrong — Grey Zone people check by hand. At the table: *“Your AI says it's 70% sure it's the blue wire.”* Then let them decide.
- **🎬 New scene** (console header) — when the scene changes (a new place, time passes, the fight is long over), tap it: on every sheet, scene-long item effects end (no crash), toy drones recharge and once-per-scene abilities (*Scrounge*) come back; the session log gets a *New scene* line. It asks first.
- **⚡ Harm** (console header, decision 274) — out-of-the-ordinary damage to one player, a few or everyone: crashing through glass, a jolt from a power unit, burning, debris, a fall. Pick from the **library** (16 presets in `gm/data/harm-presets.json`, plus *A fall*, where the height gives the dice) or fill it yourself — every field stays editable: **damage** dice (rolled or typed; *each their own* rolls for each player), **armor counts** or not, **their roll** — none, *to avoid it* (a success: nothing) or *to halve it* — and with what, a **condition** on a failure, **+1 / +2 Strain**, and a line for their card. Their sheet shows what it costs and what rolling well saves; the roll costs **no action** in a fight. Activity gets each result. A hazard's *Send … hit* and a player card's *⤓ Fall…* open the same window, already filled.
- **🎁 Give** (console header, decision 276; the players' side in 8-1) — items, cash and Credit Score to one player, several, everyone, or a **crew stash**. Search the whole catalog and your **Forge uniques** (each sent as a fresh copy), or add a **custom item** — a name and a note: a clue, a key card, "a loose panel, wires behind" — with a quantity. **Cash**: each gets it, or tick *split between them*. **Credit Score** ±, and a line on why. Players get a window they must **Take** (like a message — hard to miss); taking it logs *From the GM: …*, so the audit never flags it. A crew stash gets the items and the cash at once.
- **🏃 Chase** (console header, decision 275; the rules in 6-9) — **Set up**: who's chasing (a **starter** — mall security, a corporate response team, a gang, a bounty hunter, police, dogs, tracker drones — or one of **your saved presets**: name, how many, their chase TN, *faster* (+2), *a drone overhead*, *they may stop and shoot* with TN and damage, a note; **💾 Save as a preset** keeps it in `local/gm/chase-presets.json`), who runs, the starting gap. **Each leg**: the track (− / + to nudge it), the drone (take it out / put one up), a **complication** drawn for the stretch (🎴 Redraw, None — `gm/data/chase-complications.json`), the crew's **moves** as their sheets roll them, the pursuers' move (*Chase* or *Stop and shoot*) and roll (🎲 or type the d20), then **Resolve the leg**: the gap moves, and what it costs waits on buttons — the complication on whoever failed (⚡ Harm opens, filled), their fire on the cover-fire shooters first, **tiring** from leg 5 (+1 Strain and a CON roll, Slowed on a failure). *Leave behind* shows on anyone who failed two legs running. **⏹ Stop: to the map** ends it for a fight (put a map on the table and open Prepare); a gap of 0 or 6 ends it by itself.
- **🌦 Environment** (console header) — the scene as you tell it: a date and a time (whatever you type — nothing advances by itself), the weather (one, with its icon), the temperature in °C and any hazards (radiation, curfew, drone sweep…). *Show it* puts a thin strip on top of every sheet at the table; *Clear it* takes it off. Narrative only: no rule reads it. It stays until you change it, even if the server restarts.
- **⏱ Timer** (console header) — a countdown for a puzzle, a decision, a guard coming back: minutes and seconds, a short label, and who sees it (*Everyone* or the characters you tick). Their sheets play the *your turn* sound and show it in the strip; the button shows the time left. Tap it again for *Pause* / *Resume*, *−1 min* / *+1 min* and *Stop*. At zero the sheets say *Time's up* and your 🔔 Activity gets a line. Not during a fight: a fight starting cancels it (the sheets are told) — use a round countdown there.
- **Round countdowns** (*Fight* bookmark, *⏰ Countdowns*, during a fight) — a label and a number of rounds (*The bomb · 3*): every *End round* takes one off; at zero the log says *⏰ The bomb — now*, Activity gets a line and the countdown goes. Yours alone unless you tick *players see it*: then the sheets' strip shows *⏰ 2 rounds — The bomb*. ✕ removes one; the fight ending clears them.
- **Display** (console header) — the interface's size (Auto by the window, or A− / A+ from 85 to 120 %) and the theme — any of the players' themes, chosen for the console alone (decisions 199, 225). Handy on a laptop running many panels.
- **⚡ Event…** (in the same window) — for when the table stalls: 45 short events written to be read aloud (tension, clue, social, hazard, opportunity, the Hum, and the **daily grind** — 2045's small frictions: an ad you can't skip, a Credit Score alert that locks a door, an exit survey, a smart lock updating at the worst moment), searchable and filtered by kind; the ones you used lately sink to the bottom. Pick one and read it out (or make it yours); each comes with **three ways through** — *Hide*, *Play it cool*, *Show the paperwork* — each a roll with what happens either way, plus the best- and worst-placed classes and a twist. Tap the way the players choose: the dice become 1d20 and, if *About* is a character at the table, the TN is theirs with the difficulty applied. *Log* writes the event, the roll and the matching outcome into the session log. You choose, you narrate — nothing is sent to the players.

> **Keep it fun.** If the whole plan hangs on one roll, either make it easier or make failure open a different door. A heist that dies on a Sleight of Hand check at the front gate is a short evening.

## 9-5. Exploration and the spotlight {#sec-9-5}

**The rule.** Out of combat the game runs on intent and means (6-3): the player says what they want and how; you name the skill and the step; the dice decide how well. Time is a resource (the Operating Clock, 6-1); preparation before a job (the zero-round, 6-4) earns advantages for the fight to come. Locks follow 5-3: mechanical → Sleight of Hand, electronic → Electronics / Security, networked → Computers / Hacking, or STR to force it.

**In the console — Explore tab.** A cheat sheet for everything that isn't a fight:

- **Ask for a roll** — pick a skill or attribute (or tap its row in *Who's best at…*), tick one player or a group, add a word on why if you like, *Ask*. Each sheet gets a window in the middle of the screen with *Roll*: the usual roll window, with your table difficulty, their tools and the light. The result comes back under your ask (✓ / ✗, the die against the TN) and as a pop-up; a player can put it aside (✕) without rolling. Your table difficulty is shown in their window and they can't change it. **👥 Group check** (decision 271): tick it with two or more players — their cards say *Group check*, and when the last one has rolled, one line in Activity tallies it: the group succeeds when at least half succeeded.
- **Grant intel** (decision 197) — after legwork (a hacked file, a contact, a searched body, a debrief): pick an enemy type from your bestiary, *Known* (armor, HP, squares, weapons), *Studied* (everything, and +1 to hit that type) or *Rumour* (your own words, true or not, shown as unverified), tick who learns it, *Send*. Players also learn by the *Study* action in a fight (the server answers from your bestiary; you get a pop-up) and once from their background (7-9). Talents marked `secret` in the bestiary never reach a sheet. The manual's Collection no longer prints any numbers.
- **Who's best at…** — every skill side by side for the characters playing now, the best TN highlighted. When nobody knows who should try, you do.
- **Per character** — debt anchor, notable traits, credentials, life path and backstory. These are your hooks: a contact from their career, a creditor from their debt, a place from their youth.
- **Look and portrait** — the player's *Appearance & manner* sits under the photo (read only: it is theirs). **📷 Change** sends a new portrait straight to that sheet (it waits if the player is offline). At the bottom, *Prompts* holds the portrait and backstory prompts with a Copy button: players without an image AI can leave the picture to you. Each card shows name, class, HP, Drive and strongest skills; everything else folds (appearance, anchors, life path, backstory, prompts) and stays as you left it.

> **Keep it fun.** Give each player a moment where *their* character is the right one for the job. The Explore tab makes it easy to see who hasn't had one yet.

## 9-6. Running a fight {#sec-9-6}

**The rule.** Everyone rolls **d20 + INIT** once; the order stays for the whole fight (7-1). A combatant may *Delay* to the end of the round. Each turn has a number of actions (shown on every sheet and enemy card): move, attack, ability, reload, interact, aim, and so on (7-3). Enemy attacks are rolled exactly like the players': d20 under the attack TN, damage minus the target's armor, location attacks at −4 TN with a condition if damage gets through (7-5). Players never see enemy stats: their sheets show an anonymous order and the **Hostiles** list — letter, the name you give, a wound band (Unhurt, Hurt, Bloodied, Down) and visible conditions.

**In the console — Scene tab.** (The stages and panels are in 9-8.)

1. **Set up.** Enemies come from *Markers → Add enemies* (*Enemies* without a map): this mission's threats, saved groups, the library search (chapter 10 shows it as a collection), or a quick custom one (name, HP, armor, actions, INIT, one attack); or a map layout. With a map on the table they wait, hidden, until a fight. Connected players who aren't sitting out join by themselves. **Pictures:** an enemy with art shows a thumbnail in the order and its portrait on its card (tap it for the full picture); copies wear different looks where there are several — *◐ Look* changes one. *🤝 Ally* puts an entry on the crew's side: the sheets list it by name, in green, under *Allies*, and the challenge meter stops counting it. Players see a picture only while that enemy is on the board. The art is GM-only: drop `<id>_portrait[_n]` / `<id>_full[_n]` images into `assets/enemies_raw/` and run `node tools/enemy-art.mjs` (it writes small WebP copies to `local/gm/art/`).
2. **Prepare for a fight** (the stage bar): pick who joins, movement stops, every sheet rolls initiative in its get-ready window — the board shows *rolling… / rolled / ready*. *Roll missing initiative* covers the enemies and the players you take over; type a d20 in the Fight panel for anyone who rolled at the table; *Re-roll all* to start over.
3. **Start fight.** Sheets get the order and their turn banner.
4. **Turns.** *Now acting* shows whose turn it is. Mark each combatant *Done* (in any order); *Delay* moves one to the end of the round; *Next turn ▶* moves on. **Player hits:** when a player attacks an enemy they picked, the hit floats over the map, prefilled (who, damage, location) and editable: *Apply* runs it through that enemy's armor and location like damage you type; *Dismiss* drops it. **Cover** comes prefilled from the map on the table — the line from the shooter's marker to the enemy's when the hit arrived (hover it for the reason): partial +2 or full +4 on top of the enemy's armor, never halved or ignored, never cracking; change it if the table says otherwise. Misses show for a moment as news. Abilities land here too: a pending hit can carry conditions (ticked: Suppressed, Marked, Prone…), armor cracks and the armor rule (half / ignored), and *Apply* adds them; an ability that only needs you to do something (cover, a decoy, a reveal) shows as a note with *Done* — one that raises cover also offers *Place* (see *Pieces* below).
5. **End round.** Runs every sheet's round end: class engines tick, timed effects count down, conditions clear.
6. **End combat** (the round bar's ⋯) when it's over: with a map, the fallen become bodies and the others wait again (*After the fight*, 9-8). *Clear defeated* tidies the list mid-fight (bodies too).

**Enemy cards.** Grouped in the order you reach for them: **Fight** (HP and damage, the attack buttons, armor / move / initiative / actions, Tells), **Conditions** (the active ones; the rest under *＋ Add a condition*), **Players see**, a folded **Profile** (description, talents, immunities, darkness vision), then notes; at the foot **🏃 Flee** and **Remove**.

- **Players see** — the public name ("Guard in red"); the letter is given automatically and never reused. *Hide* keeps an enemy (an ambusher, a sniper) off the players' order and Hostiles list until you reveal it.
- **HP** — type the damage a player tells you and pick where it landed (a normal hit, or ⌖ head / chest / abdomen / arm / leg for a location attack: the condition is added for you); armor is subtracted unless you tick *ignore armor*. *Crack armor −1* when a location attack is fully stopped.
- **ACT** pips track the enemy's actions this round.
- **Attack buttons** — normal or ⌖ location attack open a **roll window**: pick who it attacks, their **cover** (the better of the map's line from this enemy and the player's own setting, both shown; tap another to change it) and body or location, then each die — *🎲 Roll* or type the die you rolled (*Use*): the d20, then on a hit the damage and, for a location attack, where it lands (with what it does). The first d20 uses one of the enemy's actions. *Send the hit* puts a card on that player's sheet: what hits them and what gets through their armor, with *Apply* or *Dodge*; the combat log tells you which they chose. A miss is just logged. An enemy's conditions change its numbers: its attack buttons and the window show the TN its own conditions leave it (Blinded −10, Suppressed −4, Focused +2), plus the target's (melee at a Prone player +4, ranged at a Blinded one +4); Enraged adds +2 to its melee damage; Slowed and Stunned cut its action pips like a player's. **Criticals** (decision 265, 7-5): a natural **1** says *💥 CRITICAL HIT* and the hit card tells the player their armor and cover don't count; a natural **20** says *FUMBLE* with one button — *Jammed* (ranged) or *Exposed* (melee) — that puts it on the enemy, or narrate it your way. A player's critical hit arrives in *Player hits* marked *💥 CRITICAL*, armor set to *ignore armor* and cover to none.
- **Injuries and conditions** — tap to set; they show on the players' Hostiles list. Each active one says how it ends (its *Clears* text, or the ability's own length — *to the end of next round*), and it comes off by itself when that moment comes: the log says what wore off (*… is done · wore off: B Blinded*, *— End of round 2 — · wore off: …*). Tap it to end it early; set it again to keep it longer (decision 236).
- **🏃 Flee** — the enemy gets away: its marker leaves the map and it goes back to **Waiting**, hidden, with its HP, armor and conditions as they were. A later fight can bring it back (*Join*, or tick it in Prepare), and meeting it again on the map is a new contact. **Remove** is for good.
- **Sees in the dark** — night vision or thermal: the dark doesn't hide anyone from it (7-2 §4).
- **Notes** — position, target, tricks. A Scavenger's **Tells** on that enemy show here too.

**The challenge meter** estimates how hard the fight is from both sides' damage per round, armor and HP: **Easy, Fair, Hard, Deadly**, with notes ("armor nobody can get through", "one-round knockout risk"). It ignores abilities, cover and tactics, so treat it as a first opinion. The rating when the fight starts also decides the danger XP (9-11).

**↶ Undo** on the tracker takes back your last changes (about 15) and is announced to the table as "The GM took back their last move" — no names, so hidden enemies stay hidden.

> **Keep it fun.** The meter can't see the room. If a Deadly fight is a slog after two rounds, the enemies' morale breaks; if an Easy one is over in a turn and the players look bored, reinforcements arrive. A fight should end when its question is answered — who controls the room, did they get the data — not when the last HP is gone.

Slum ghettos can be walked (slowly) but not seen through; parks, plazas, parking lots, markets and landmarks are open ground. Set these maps to *bright*: light rules don't work at this scale. > **Keep it fun.** A map is a stage, not a puzzle. Build the rooms where something will happen, and describe the rest. Ten minutes on a good map beats an hour on a perfect one.

## 9-8. The map on the table {#sec-9-8}

**The rule.** Movement, line of sight and range follow 7-2. The map *informs*; you *decide*. Line of sight and range shown on a tablet are a guide — you confirm cover and anything unusual.

**How it plays.** Players see an **Active map** tab while a map is on the table (7-2 §5). They see what their crew can see right now, and remember what they've seen. They drag their own marker: free out of combat; in combat one Move action gives them their squares for the round. They open and close doors next to them (an Interact action in combat). A locked door stays shut: they're told it's locked, you get a note that they tried. Torches, flares and vision gear work on their own.

**The Scene tab — stages.** A **stage bar** on top — *Setup → Explore → Prepare → Fight → After the fight* — saying where the table is, what is happening and the next step's button (*Start exploring*, *Prepare for a fight*, *Back to exploring*). A map going on starts Setup, and **players can't move until you press *Start exploring*** (their map says movement is off, so they'll ask if you forget); **⟲ Back to setup** holds them again — to change the layout, say. A fight starting or ending moves the stage by itself. The map on the table and the fight are kept by the table server, not by your browser: reload the console or open it on another computer and they are as you left them (two consoles stay in step).

**Prepare for a fight** (Scene tab) stops the table: movement stops (a move that arrives after the stop goes back, and the player is told why), and every sheet in the fight gets the get-ready window — weapons, abilities, and **initiative**: *I'm ready* stays locked until they rolled (or typed the die they rolled at the table). The bar shows a board: each player *rolling… / rolled 14 / ready ✓*. **Take over** closes one player's window (away from the table, stuck): you roll for them. **Roll missing initiative** rolls for the enemies and the players you took over — never for someone still rolling. **Start fight** opens when everyone is ready or taken over; a player who got ready isn't asked again when the fight starts. **Call off** closes every window.

**Head start** (Scene tab, decision 233 — the Prep Phase of 6-4). When the crew has time — they hear the patrol coming, or they saw the enemies first (the Contact alert offers it too) — press **Head start…** instead of Prepare: pick 1–3 prep actions and, if the enemies are completely unaware, *Complete surprise*; then who joins, as in Prepare — they join **revealed**, since the crew knows they're there (*Hide* on a card if one should stay unseen). The fight opens at **round 0**: every sheet gets those prep actions, all at once (no turn order; *Move* walks squares), the enemies don't act, and a newly spotted enemy is only noted (nothing stops). Engines and stances set now carry into round 1 — no round-end tick in between. **End head start → initiative** (or **Alarm!**, a failed prep check) opens the initiative window on every sheet (the roll only); then *Start fight* begins round 1 in initiative order, with a fresh pool. With complete surprise, round 1 says *Surprise round: the enemies can't take reactions* on your Fight panel and on the sheets. Never pressed, nothing changes: Explore → Prepare → Fight as usual.

**After the fight** (Scene tab). *End combat* with a map on the table: every sheet leaves combat; the **fallen become bodies** — a body outline on their square, its GM label the enemy's name and its GM note the enemy's notes (what they carry, what the crew finds: a live note on the map, remove it like any piece); the **ones still standing wait again** where they are, already seen (no new contact; a later Prepare ticks them). **↶ Rewind fight** puts the map, the enemies and every player who fought back to how they were when the fight started — for a fight that shouldn't have happened; it is offered until the next fight starts or the map comes off. **Clear enemies** (the Fight bookmark's Setup) takes every enemy off the tracker and the map.

**Groups and layouts** (Scene tab). A **group** only says who comes together: when one member is spotted, Prepare ticks the others too (*same group: Gang A*) — you confirm or untick. Groups are made two ways. **Saved groups** sit in *Markers → Add enemies*, after this mission's threats (groups made only of mission threats come first, marked *mission*): **Recall** brings the members in, waiting, on the *Not on the map* list to place. **On the fly**: *Groups…* on the Waiting list — tick enemies already in the scene, name the group (one radios the others) and, if you like, *Keep as a reusable group*. A **layout** is where the enemies wait on one map: place them, then *Save layout…* (the bar, in Setup). *Load layout…* puts one back on the map; putting a map on the table offers *Fresh, with a layout*; **⟲ Reload layout** resets the room — every enemy off, the layout back as saved. Enemies can still be added at any moment (the library search marks this mission's threats), and late joiners still *Join* a running fight.

**⚖ Plan difficulty** (Scene tab, whenever no fight is being set up or run) — how hard would this fight be? Tick any of the crew (the ones at the table come ticked; every character the table knows is listed), fill the opposition from *This scene's enemies*, a saved **group**, one of this map's **layouts** or single library enemies, adjust the counts, pick the table difficulty: the same challenge meter as the Fight bookmark, with its reasons. Nothing at the table changes until you press **Deploy these enemies** (decision 246): with a map on the table they wait, hidden, in *Markers → Not on the map*, ready to place; without one they go into the fight's setup. *Use this difficulty at the table* sets the difficulty you planned with.

**In the console — Scene tab, the panels.** The map in the middle, everything else a bookmark at its sides. It works **without a map** too: *Fight* (light and difficulty, the tracker), *Undo* and *Ask a roll* on the left, *Selected*, *Players* and **Enemies** (add enemies — this mission's threats, groups, the library, a quick custom one) on the right — so a fight with no map still runs from here.

- **Put a map on the table** — pick a saved map; it fills the page. **It opens hidden from the players** (decision 246): no Map or Tactical tab on their sheets, nothing on the TV, nobody gets spotted — so you can place the enemies and the crew in peace. *Players see the map* in the **Table** bookmark shows it; *Start exploring*, *Prepare* and a *Head start* show it too — Prepare is the sudden “you are entering combat” moment. The map's header says *hidden from the players* while it is. The rest are **bookmarks** at its sides (decision 190): on the left *Fight*, *Undo*, *Ask a roll*, *Security* and *Site alert*; on the right *Selected*, *Players*, *Markers*, *Fog & light*, *Marks* and *Table* (players' moves, another map, save, take it off). One panel per side slides over the map's edge; a fight starting opens *Fight*, tapping a marker, piece, door or zone opens *Selected*.
- **Fight** (bookmark on the left) — the whole combat tracker beside the map, so a fight runs from here without switching tabs. On top, one line for the **light** (*☀ Bright* / *☾ Dark*, the map's environment) and the **table difficulty** (a dropdown, the same as the Table tab's). Before the fight, a folding **Setup**: *Roll missing initiative*, *Re-roll all*, *Clear enemies* (initiative is asked and the fight started from the stage bar's Prepare). During it, the **round bar**: round, who acts, *Next turn ▶* (or *End round*), *↶ Undo*, and **⋯** for *End round*, *Clear defeated* and *End combat*. Under it the challenge in one line (tap it for the reasons), the initiative cards (d20, *Delay*, *Done ✓*), and the log, folded to its last line. **Tap a card** and that fighter's full card opens in *Selected* on the right — HP, damage, conditions, the attack buttons with their roll window — and their marker is picked on the map; tapping a marker does the same. **Player hits** float over the bottom of the map while any wait: *Apply* or *Dismiss* as on the Combat tab.
- **Undo** (bookmark on the left) — every undo in one place: the combat tracker's last change, and for each player in the fight (or at the table) *Last action*, *This round*, *Whole combat* or *Back to a line…*; the Undo log folds at the bottom. The same undos as the player cards' ↶, which stay where they are.
- **Add enemies** (top of *Markers*) — **This mission**: the live mission's threats from the Story tab, one tap each (the pill says how many are already in); below, a search of the whole library — and, with the search empty, the whole library **by faction** (decision 257: The Watch, Obsidian, the Syndicate… Street, Rogue machines, Wildlife & strays), each folded, elites first, each enemy with its role (grunt, sniper, heavy, drone, leader…); *Copies* adds several at once. ⚖ Plan difficulty's list is grouped the same way. Factions and roles live in the library file (`factions` at the top, `faction` and `role` on each enemy). New enemies wait under *Not on the map*.
- **Not on the map** — players and enemies waiting to be placed: tap one, then a square. Drag markers to move them; tap one marker then another to check sight. Under it, **Players**: everyone at the table and where their marker is — *Place* (or *Move*) puts any of them on a square, even one whose marker got lost; **↻ Refresh** asks every sheet to re-sync and reads who is at the table again (a player who joined late, a tablet that reconnected).
- **Pieces** (in *Markers*, decision 238) — put a piece down during play: cover the crew raises, a crate kicked over, anything from the library. The usual cover comes first (*Scrap cover*, crates, rubble, each with the cover it gives); the search finds the rest. Tap one, then a square: it lands there, seen by the players, and opens in *Selected* to turn, move, hide or remove. When a player's ability raises cover (*Improvised Cover*), its note on the hit card has **Place Scrap cover**: tap it, then the square next to them — the card clears and the plate carries their name in its GM label.
- **Locked gates** (decision 248) — any piece that opens (a chain-link gate, a hatch, a container, a safe) can be **Locked**: in the Maps tab (*Lock* when it's selected) or live (*Lock* / *Unlock* in *Selected*). Players open unlocked ones like doors; a locked one tells them so, and your Activity says who tried. Players never see the lock.
- **Overwatch** (decision 250) — a player on overwatch shows on your map as a faint **quarter circle** (the squares they watch). Drag an enemy into or through it and the console asks *… is watching E — let them shoot?*: **Shoot** sends their sheet the call (they shoot, free, or let it pass); *Not now* does nothing. Nothing fires by itself. Without a map — or a crossing you missed — the Fight bookmark lists *On overwatch* with **Grant the shot** at an enemy you pick. It ends at the start of their next turn, with the shot, or with the fight.
- **Players' switches** (decision 241) — in a fight, on their turn, a player can spend an Interact action on a wall switch or a device within reach of their marker (lamps, screens, machines; not ceiling lights, fires, street lamps or lifts). The console checks it (next to it, their turn), switches it — the power wiring does the rest, lights and fog included — and your 🔔 Activity says *Kai switched off the Floor lamp*. A breaker or a power source only asks: the line is waiting in Activity and the piece is selected, so you switch it from *Selected* (or don't). To put something back, tap it and use *Selected* like any piece. Outside a fight players can't switch anything: that stays yours. **Locks** (decision 255): a player who tries a locked door or piece is told it's locked and their sheet remembers it; afterwards they tap *Locked — try to open it* — no action spent — and Activity says *Kai wants to open the locked door — ask for a roll*, with the door selected: ask for the roll (🎯) and decide what the attempt costs. If you unlocked it meanwhile, their sheet is told and forgets the lock.
- **Waiting enemies** — enemies you add with a map on the table and no fight running **wait**: hidden from the players, out of the initiative, the challenge and the players' Hostiles list. Place them where they lurk; they are listed under **Waiting** in the *Fight* bookmark (tap one for its card, ✕ to remove it) and they are **saved with the map's scene** — a saved scene (or the autosave) brings them back. **Contact:** the moment a player's marker sees a waiting enemy (the same sight as the fog: walls, doors, light, thermal), players stop moving, every sheet shows *Contact!*, and the *Fight* bookmark opens with who saw whom: **Prepare the fight…** or **Let them carry on** (movement back on; that enemy won't raise it again). **Prepare the fight** only picks **who joins**: the enemies the players saw are ticked and revealed, the others unticked — tick one to have it join hidden (an ambusher). *Add to the fight* puts them in the fight; players stay stopped while everyone gets ready (Prepare) and you *Start fight*. The rest keep waiting: *Join* on the Waiting list adds one to the Setup, or during a fight as a reinforcement; *Pick who joins…* opens the same window without a contact. Waiting enemies roll initiative only once they join, and a finished fight leaves no rolls behind. Without a map, every enemy you add joins the setup straight away.
- **Players move their markers: on / off** — let players drag their own markers, or move everyone yourself. In combat the console re-checks every move against the player's squares and turn.
- **Fog of war & light** — fog *On* or *Off — they see everything*; *Reveal all*, *Reset*, and *Edit squares* to reveal a square by hand (a floor plan they bought, a camera feed). *Bright* / *Dark* changes the environment live.
- **Pieces** — tap one to reveal it, hide it again, switch it on or off (lights, sensors, lasers), open or lock a door, wreck a vehicle (or make it intact), **✥ Move** it (then tap the square it goes to) or turn it ⟲ ⟳ — a car pulling up, a barricade dragged across the alley — or take it off for this session. Its GM label and note show here too, and you can edit them. The saved map keeps its starting state.
- **Weak walls** — tap a breakable wall: *Break through* turns it into a breach for everyone (nothing blocks movement or sight there; *Repair* if you tapped the wrong one), and *Show cracks to players* hints it on their map when a character notices. The roll to break it is your call.
- **Event zones** — tap one to read its label and note.
- **Hazards on the table** (decision 258) — tap one: *Turn off / on*, *Show to players / Hide*. When a marker **steps into** an active hazard, **walks through** one on the way (*went through*), or **starts a turn** in one during a fight — your own drag of a player's marker counts too — your 🔔 Activity says so — *Kai stepped into H2 · ♨ Steam (Boiler leak) · 1d6* — with **Send Steam hit**: the **⚡ Harm** window, filled from the zone (its dice; their roll *to halve it* with Avoid / Reflexes — change anything), then *Send* (cancel and the line keeps waiting); ignore it if it shouldn't hurt. An enemy in a hazard gets the line too, without the button (apply it on their card). A player who works the control piece (Interact on a valve or a breaker, in or out of a fight) asks you: the line offers the roll (*🎯 Ask Mechanics & Fabrication*, *Electronics & Security* for electric) and *Turn H2 off* — tap it if the roll succeeds. A wall switch that controls a hazard is flipped as usual and its line offers the off / on.
- **Loot on the table** (decision 277) — when a player opens or searches a piece with loot, what's in plain sight becomes a **stash** named after the piece, on every sheet (*🔍 … · found here*), and they get a private line saying what's inside; it goes once it's empty. Activity tells you, and when there's a **hidden part** the line waits on you: *🎯 Ask Perception* (or whatever you set) and **🔍 Reveal the hidden part** — tap it when they find it (a clue there is your cue for a 🧩 puzzle). *Selected* shows a piece's loot, with *📦 Open it for them* and *Reveal the hidden part*. Taking the map off the table puts what's left back into the pieces — and into the save.
- **People on the map** — the ones not fighting: pick a role (worker, clerk, guard…) and *+ Add*: players see *Worker 1* until you rename it to a real name. Each has a private note for you. **Party** is the role for several characters moving as one on a big map (a district, a landscape): *Party 1 — Kira & Jordan*. Tap one on the map: *Take off the map* lifts its marker (it stays in the list), **Remove** takes it away for good. Not to be confused with enemy **groups** (who comes together into a fight) or the Maps tab's *Group* tool (pieces moved together).
- **Marks** — players point things out to each other in their colour (a crosshair, an arrow, a question mark, a freehand line, 7-2 §5); they fade after two minutes unless pinned. Pick a tool to draw your own, in amber ("you hear it from *here*"); in marking mode a tap on any mark removes it; *Clear all* wipes the board. Marks are never saved and never touch the rules; they go when the map comes off the table.
- **Saved scenes** (decision 232) — taking a map off the table asks: **Save and take it off** under a name (filled in: map — mission — date) or **Take it off without saving** (it goes into the map's single **Autosave**, overwritten each time). A saved scene keeps the whole situation: enemies (waiting, hurt, bodies), revealed pins and captions, doors, breaches, moved or wrecked pieces, lights, every NPC and **Party** marker, the fog, your labels and notes — never the players' sheets. Putting a map on lists **Fresh** (as you built it, no enemies) and its saved scenes, each with its date and a line like *5 enemies waiting · 2 bodies · fog 40 % revealed · 3 crew placed*; ✕ deletes one. *Save scene as…* (Table bookmark) saves at any moment. A map can't come off mid-fight: end it (or rewind it) first. Players who aren't here aren't drawn; their spots come back when they are. Handy for a city or district map the crew keeps returning to: move a **Party** marker (an NPC role: *Party 1 — Kira & Jordan*) when the crew splits.
- **The TV screen** — the table server prints a TV address ending in **/screen**. Open it in a browser tab and cast that tab to the TV: a bar on top and the map full screen, as the crew sees it together — every player's marker, what any of them sees, bright or dark, everyone's marks. It only watches: nothing on it changes the table, and it never shows messages, the fight's numbers or anything you keep hidden. Pan and zoom it like any map; *Fit*, *Full screen* and *Day / Night* sit in its bar (the other buttons are for later). It follows whatever map is on the table.
- **Links** — tap a link: *Reveal* / *Hide* it, *Cut it* (players see a red cross on it: the power line is down, the road is blocked) or *Restore it*. Players' Map tab lists the kinds of link on the map.
- **Power** — tap a breaker: *Trip* / *Reset*; a switch: *Switch off* / *on*; a source: *Cut the power* / *Restore power*. You're told first what it does ("Turns off: Ceiling light ×3 · Locks: door D1"). A wired piece or electric door lists the cables into it, each with *Cut the cable* / *Repair the cable*, and says whether it has power. Lights go dark for everyone, sensors stop, electric doors lock or unlock by their mode; players only see what happens.
- **Security board** (bookmark on the left, on maps with a network or security devices) — what the security room sees right now: each node and device *reporting*, *offline* (and where it was cut), *fooled* (false data), *no power* or *not networked*, with how long ("since round 3" in a fight). Tap one to act on it: **Take offline / Bring online** (the cable cut, the router shut down — everything behind it stops reporting) and **Tamper / Clean** (a looped camera, a spoofed access point — everything behind it reports false data). Both are shown before they happen. Players see nothing of it: you narrate who comes down to check, and when.
- **Site alert** (🚨 bookmark on the left, decision 268) — **one meter for the whole map**: how much the site's security (its networks, its AI, the people reading the streams) is paying attention. It replaces the old per-network Trace. Tap a level — *Calm*, *Twitchy*, *Alert*, *Lockdown* — or trim the number with − / +; it **never drops by itself**. Under the levels, a reminder of how the site behaves at this one (Twitchy: a technician reads the logs, a guard is asked to check in; Alert: nobody wanders, patrols in pairs, a team with a tech heads where it was heard; Lockdown: sealed, keys change, the response is on its way). What ticks it is yours — a guard who stops checking in, a zone going dark, a forced door, gunfire near a mic; the lock lines in Activity carry a *+1 Trace* button. **By itself** only what the networks hear: a hacker's **ping** adds 1 on a success, 2 + the network's security on a failure (Civilian +0, Corporate +1, Military / AI-run +2 — set on its uplink), counted once per ping; a toy drone (6-4) adds 1. A level reached that way lands in Activity. **What it costs the players:** on Alert, Stealth, Sleight of Hand, Electronics & Security, Computers & Hacking and the Hacker's Class TN are −1 for anyone on this map (−2 on Lockdown); their check window shows the row, and only Alert and Lockdown ever reach their sheets — Calm and Twitchy stay yours. **One-tap consequences**, offered when they fit and never applied by themselves: *Arm the sensors* (from Twitchy: sensors and laser lines switched off go on) and *Lock the networked doors* (from Alert: every electric door on a network). Below, each network: its security and the node that last heard a ping; on Lockdown, *Re-key* wipes the players' list of it. A ping rolled on a sheet comes here; the console lists what hangs on each network within 10 squares of their marker and sends it back to the sheet — types only, never positions (a node's *Network name*, set in the Maps tab, shows as it is). *Hold ping results for my OK* keeps each result here until you *Send* or *Discard* it. A player's toy drone sends you one line per flight — recon or decoy, and how it went: tell them what it saw.
- **Sensors that notice** (decision 269) — the map's sensors now watch, and tell **you**, never the players. A **camera** covers an arc in front of it (directional: 90°, aim it by turning it in the Maps tab; 360° cameras and omni nodes: all round) out to its range — 8 squares directional, 5 all round, *Camera range* − / + in the Maps tab — only squares it can see (walls, closed doors) and not dark ones. **Motion** sensors watch their line ahead, **audio** their circle. When a player's marker steps into a sensor's coverage you get a line in Activity — *🎥 Tess in view of the Camera “Back corridor” (network A) — your call* — with *+1 Trace* and *🎯 Ask Stealth*; once per sensor until they leave its coverage. A **laser line that is on** gives a line every time someone crosses it, hidden or not. A sensor that is off, wrecked, or on a network that is cut, looped or without power says nothing — so hacking a camera off before walking past it is real play. Nothing happens by itself: the line is information, the cost is yours. *Fog & light → Sensor coverage* shades what the working sensors cover, on your map only.
- **Skyline** — on a city map, *Skyline* shows your screen the city at an angle (*Top-down* goes back); tap a district to select it. It changes only your screen: each player chooses on their own sheet.
- **Take the map off the table** when the scene ends.

> **Keep it fun.** If the map and the story disagree, the story wins: move the marker, open the door, reveal the room. If a player struggles with dragging, move their marker for them. The map is there to end arguments, not to start them.

## 9-9. Missions, the session log and factions {#sec-9-9}

**How it works.** The **Story tab** is the campaign's notebook, saved on your computer (`local/gm/campaign.json`) and never published.

- **Missions** — title, code, **objectives** (primary and secondary, marked done or failed), **chapters** for the scenes (*Next chapter ▶*), the zones involved.
- **The crew's case** (decisions 278–279; the players' side in 6-10) — at the bottom of the Story tab, **🗂 Case** for a mission (or General). Players see the **active and finished** missions. **Objectives**: the main one always; a secondary one only when you tick *shown to players* on it (marking it done never reveals it). **The dossier** fills by itself with what you send to **everyone** (handouts, puzzles, table-wide messages — private ones stay private); **👤 Reveal a person** / **📍 Reveal a place** adds a name, what they know, a handout picture; ✕ takes an entry out. **The board** is the crew's: you see it as they do, choose **who holds the pen** (they pass it among themselves), move / link / unpin any card, and pin **📨 anonymous tips** (with any handout from your folder as a picture — it's shared with them). Suggestions and handovers reach Activity. Kept on the table server (`local/table/cases.json`).
- **Notable NPCs** — shared across missions, with their state.
- **Threats** — prepared enemies; *→ Combat* sends them to the tracker.
- **Maps** — link the mission's saved maps; *Edit* opens one in the Maps tab, *On the table* puts it on the Scene tab.
- **Session log** — 15 lines a page; anything you write, every *🎲 Roll / note*, and every line you pin with 📌 from **At the table** (the live table log).
- **Handouts** — the **🖼 Handout** button in the console header, on any tab, opens images from `local/gm/handouts/`: pick one, tick who gets it (or *Everyone*), add a caption. It arrives on their sheets like a message.
- **AI prompts** — *Build prompt* writes a debrief of the mission (from the log and objectives) or a draft for the next one, to paste into your own AI. *Save to campaign story* keeps the debrief in the campaign story.

**Factions.** The rule: each power has a standing from **−3 Hostile** to **+3 Patron** (−2 Watched, −1 Wary, 0 Neutral, +1 Friendly, +2 Allied). It moves by 1, always with a reason, inside a floor and a ceiling you set ("they'll never trust a crew that hit their depot"). In play it decides who answers a call, what a contact will risk, who shows up uninvited. In the console it sits under the campaign; changes go into the session log, and the AI prompts carry the standings. Players never see it.

**The Notes tab** (Prep, decision 281). Your Markdown, in the console: your own notes in `local/gm/notes/` (＋ *New note*; folders with `/`, e.g. `npcs/rask`) and the campaign documents beside them — the campaign bible, the intrigue web, the mission briefs — edited in place. **Edit + preview** side by side (on a tablet, one at a time), an **outline** from the headings to jump around, and **autosave** a moment after you stop typing (Ctrl+S saves at once). If a file changed somewhere else while you wrote (another console, or edited on disk), you choose: keep yours, or load the other one — yours is then kept in `notes/conflicts/`. The text before each save is kept in `notes/.history/`; 🗑 moves one of your notes there too (campaign documents can't be removed from here). **🔎 The lens**: type a name or a term — *Vance Vane*, *Kestrel*, *the Book* — and see the sections named after it first, then every passage in every file that mentions it, with the heading it sits under; ↗ opens the file at that line. It's the quickest way to answer "what does the crew have on this person?". Never published, never sent to the table.

## 9-10. Crew and Unity {#sec-9-10}

**The rule.** The crew has one shared **Crew track** from 0 to 10 (start 5). It measures **whether they act together, never whether the act was good** (6-6). +1 when the crew commits to a costly or risky call together, or when you see them cover for each other; −1 when one player overrides a crew that objected. Bands: Broken 0, Fractured 1–3, Steady 4–6, Tight 7–10.

**Unity Checks.** When one character wants to do something the crew may not back — kill the witness, take the side deal — talk it through, then open a check. Everyone but the actor votes in secret on their sheet: *Back*, *Object*, *Stay out*. You see who voted what; the table sees only the tally. The actor always decides; the track records it.

**Drives.** Each character has a Drive — Justice or Ambition — and a line in their own words. It's a compass, never a score. Acting on it at a real cost is a good reason for Spotlight XP.

**In the console — Table tab, Crew & Unity.** The track with *+1 intent* and *−1*; *Open Unity Check* (actor and intent), *Reveal the tally*, then *Backed together +1*, *Override −1* or *No change · close*. Voting by voice works too: just record the outcome.

## 9-11. Experience and levels {#sec-9-11}

**The rule.** No XP per kill. Per mission, each player present gets:

- **3** for a primary objective achieved, **1** for a secondary, **1** for an objective failed (you learn from it);
- **+1** for surviving a fight rated Hard, **+2** for Deadly (the rating when the fight started);
- **+1 Spotlight** from you, at most once or twice per player per session: a great scene, a real cost paid for their Drive.

**10 XP is a level**: a talent point every level, +1 to an attribute on even levels, an extra ability slot at 3, 6 and 9 (5-9). Players spend levels only when you open level-up — usually in downtime, somewhere quiet.

**In the console.**

- **Mission XP** (Story tab) — totals per player from objectives, danger and spotlight; *Award* sends them once per mission.
- **Open level-up for everyone** / *Close level-up* (Table tab), or per player on their card.
- **Gift** a talent from the player's card: free, no prerequisites. Use it for story rewards — training with a veteran, a debt repaid in kind.
- **Washer deal** (player's card): offer to wash cash into Credit Score or cash it out — direction, cash per point, commission %, at most how many points, and a line from the fixer. It waits on their sheet (Gear) until they take it or walk away; the log explains the change to your audit (8-1).

## 9-12. Downtime and debt {#sec-9-12}

**The rule.** Between missions, somewhere quiet, time passes (5-10). Injuries heal and armor is repaired. Lifestyle upkeep is **shown, not charged** — the table settles it. Each character's **debt pressure** rises by 1 (0–3): Squeeze, Escalation, then **Crisis**, which is a mission hook. An instalment or a story resolution lowers it.

**How it plays.** Each character gets one **story event** — a creditor's visit, an old contact, a quiet moment — and the crew may get one together. Events are narrative: their options are shown as choices, never applied by the app. You and the player decide what happens.

**In the console — Story tab → Downtime.** Choose the duration. For each player the console suggests events with a **confidence %** and the reasons (their debt and its stage, career, youth, current state, the world); recently used ones fade. Pick, edit, swap, write your own or skip; add a crew event; open level-up if you like; send. Each sheet restores itself, raises the pressure, adds the event to *Story so far* and shows a report. Set pressure by hand from the player's card when the story pays a debt down.

## 9-13. Loot, the Forge and backstories {#sec-9-13}

**Street prices.** Gear bought officially costs Credit Score (traced); on the street it costs cash at a markup of **×3 to ×10** (untraced). The player picks the markup in the purchase window — you tell them which: ×3 from a friendly fence, ×10 for a rifle the night before a job. The rarest gear is street only.

**The Forge (Admin tab).** For the one-off find: pick a kind and a base item, its **icon** (what its type gives, or any other from the set — *↺ by type* goes back), change its numbers, add bonuses while it's in use (any attribute or skill, armor, actions, OFF, initiative), a one-line note, a value or *can't be fenced*, and *Forge & send* it to the player who found it. It shows in purple on their sheet and keeps its stats if given away. Forged items are kept for reuse (*Forged so far*).

**Crew stashes (Admin tab, above the Forge).** Set up a stash the crew shares (the van, a lock-up). Players put in and take out from their Sheet (Inventory); every move goes in its history, which everyone sees. *Lock* when it matters (watched, impounded, across town): players see it but can't touch it. *Raid* empties it — the history says what was lost. *Remove* hides it; it stays on disk. Their own private stashes show on their player card (📦).

**Backstories (Admin tab).** Players can ask you to write or polish their backstory from the builder or the Dossier. Requests wait here; add a note per request (hooks, ties to the campaign). In Claude Code, `/backstories` writes the waiting ones; you read, edit and send. Nothing reaches a player until you send it. A request sent twice or by mistake: **Cancel request** (decision 262) — if it replaced a backstory already written, that one comes back; otherwise it goes under *Cancelled*, where *Ask again* revives it.

> **Keep it fun.** Loot is story. A unique item works best with a name, a past and a note — "the grip is wrapped in a dead courier's scarf" — and a small bonus. A +5 is a solved problem; a +1 with a story is a conversation piece.

## 9-14. Mistakes, undo and the archive {#sec-9-14}

**The rule.** Players never undo their own sheets — they ask, and you decide.

**In the console.**

- **↶** (Undo) on a player's card — *Last action*, *This round*, *Whole combat*, or back to before any line of their log. The sheet returns to the snapshot taken just before; rolls stay in the log. Every undo is listed in the **Undo log** (Table tab), with a count on each card, so the table can see it was fair.
- **↶ Undo** on the combat tracker for your own changes, announced to the table (9-6).
- **Admin tab** — characters, missions and session logs on disk. *Archive* moves a finished character or mission out of the way; *Restore* brings it back; *Inspect* reads it. Nothing is ever deleted, and every action asks first. A player who deletes their own character at the table sends it here too (decision 239). At the very bottom, **Commands** (decision 262) lists what Claude Code (`/backstories`, `/next-mission`) and the terminal (`npm run table`, enemy art, the Collection export…) can do, one line each.

> **Keep it fun.** Undo is for mistakes — a wrong button, a misread rule, damage applied twice, a fight started too early. It is also for the rare moment when the dice produce something nobody at the table wants: a character dying to a stray bullet in a scene that didn't matter. Use that one sparingly and openly. Tell the table you're doing it and why; then it's a gift, not a cheat.

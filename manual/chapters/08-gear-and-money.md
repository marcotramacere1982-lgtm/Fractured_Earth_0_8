# Gear & Money {#ch-8}

## 8-1. Paying for Things {#sec-8-1}

*Every piece of gear has its own silhouette — a pistol, an SMG, a scoped rifle, a pistol magazine or a power cell, a vest or a helmet, pills, a hypo, lockpicks, a torch — in the market, in your inventory and on the sheet's folded sections, so you find things at a glance. (Icons from game-icons.net, CC BY 3.0.)*

Every purchase can be made in two ways, and the choice matters.

- **Official channels — Credit Score.** Corporate shops never take your money; they extend you debt. The item's Credit Score cost is subtracted from your Credit Score, and the purchase is **logged against your identity**. Cheap for groceries and rent, ruinous for anything that matters on a job: the more attention an item draws, the steeper its cost.
- **The street — physical cash.** Untraceable, illegal, and expensive: the listed price, from **no markup to ×10**, depending on local risk — the GM sets the markup for the whole table (×3 when nobody set it). No trace.

**Essentials.** The market opens on its *Essentials* tab: the cheapest gear that covers every base before going out — a gun, a blade, something to wear, a trauma kit, a prepaid burner phone, an ID Scramble Kit, a torch, rope and a multi-tool — each with what it's for, and a ✓ on what you already carry. Specialist tools (lockpicks, RFID kits…) are in their own tabs.

**Rarity** runs from **F** (everyday) to **A** (prototype). The two rarest tiers, **A and B**, never appear in official shops: street cash only.

> Credit is the leash the system uses to keep the lower classes compliant. A corporate sweep team simply gets issued its equipment; you will have to find yours.

### Your stash {#sec-8-stash}

Not everything has to be on you. Sheet → Inventory → **Stashes** (each stash folded to one line until you tap it): name the place (a locker at the bus depot, a loose tile under the sink) and 📦 on an item puts one of it there; cash goes in and out by the amount. What is stashed is not carried: it can't be lost, searched, taken or used until you go back for it — which, at the table, means the GM agrees you can get there. Out of reach during a fight. Only you and the GM see it; every move is logged.

**Crew stashes.** At the table the GM can set up stashes the whole crew shares — the van, a lock-up, a dead drop. They show on everyone's Gear tab: anyone can put things in (📦 asks which stash) or take them out, and the stash's history shows who did what. Trust is the point. The GM can **lock** one (you see it, you can't reach it: it's being watched, or the van is impounded) or **raid** it (everything in it is gone).

### What the GM gives you {#sec-8-gm-gives}

Pay from a job, a key card from a contact, the cash in a dead man's jacket: when the GM hands you something it arrives as a window on your sheet — **From the GM**, with the items, the cash, any change to your Credit Score and a line on why. Nothing is yours until you tap **Take it**; then it's in your inventory and your log says where it came from. (A fine works the same way: Credit Score down, the reason beside it.) Things for the whole crew can go straight into a crew stash instead.

### Searching and containers {#sec-8-loot}

On the table map, crates, lockers, safes and car boots can hold things. **Open** one next to you, or **🔍 Search** a piece the GM marked as searchable (*Interact* — an action in a fight). What's inside turns up as a stash everyone at the table can see — *🔍 Locker · found here* — and anyone takes what they want, as with a crew stash; it disappears once it's empty. Some things aren't in plain sight: the GM may ask for a roll (Perception, often) to find what's tucked away — and what you find may be a clue rather than gear. Whatever you leave behind stays there. When it's opened for you, a **window** shows what's inside straight away (decision 320): each thing with its note, **Take**, **Give…** (take it and hand it to someone), the cash, **Take everything** — no digging through your stashes.

**Loot** (decision 317) is what you find or steal and can't buy: junk from a dumpster, materials stripped from ruins, barter goods out of drawers and clinics, valuables lifted from flats and offices, contraband. It sits in its own *Loot & valuables* group, stacks, goes into stashes and passes between players like anything else; some missions ask you to bring a few back. A fence pays **half its street value** (*Sell*). Some things are **📡 traced** — a passport, a staff keycard, AR glasses, a phone: hot by themselves, because the system can trace them back to their owner. Carrying them is a risk the GM will remember.

::table[loot]

### Haggling {#sec-8-haggle}

A price is an opening offer. **🤝 Haggle** — on a fixer's offer, or **Haggle on the street** beside *Open market* — is **one roll**: pick how hard you push and how.

::table[haggle]

- **Haggle honestly** (Persuasion) or **work the street** (Streetwise): fail and nothing changes; a **natural 20** and the price gets 10% worse.
- **Swindle** (Deception): **double** the cut — but fail and the deal is off (on the street: the dealers know your face, 10% worse).
- A fixer's **mood** (the GM's) makes it one step easier or harder.

On a fixer's offer it moves that deal — washing, cashing out, cleaning a gun — once. On the street it lasts the **trip**, until the next scene: what you pay *and* what a fence pays you. Official purchases (Credit Score) don't haggle: the system has fixed prices.

### Bodies, locked guns and hot goods {#sec-8-bodies}

The fallen keep what they carried: some cash, often their weapon and a magazine, whatever was in their pockets. **Search** a body like a container (*Interact* next to it). Don't count on the good guns: the better a weapon, the more likely it was wrecked in the fight, bricked by its owner, or lost — a junk pistol always survives, a top-grade rifle almost never.

When one does, it is rarely a gift. The Watch's and corporate officers' guns carry a **🔒 smart lock**, keyed to their owner; the TRF's a **biometric lock** that bricks itself when its operator falls. A locked gun **won't fire**. A smart lock can be broken on the spot — **🔓 Break the lock** in your inventory, a Hard Hacking roll and an action, and the site notices whether you manage or not. A biometric one can't: only a washer brings it back.

They are also **📡 tracked** — they report where they are. While you carry one unshielded, the site you're on **stays at least on Alert**; a **Shielded Duffel** blocks the signal. And they are **🔥 hot**: a fence pays only a quarter (traced loot too), and being caught with one is the kind of thing the GM remembers. Between missions a **washer** can clean one: unlocked, untracked, a clean history — for a share of its price, and a roll to see whether the trail leads back to you.

> **Never touch a smart weapon without a plan.** Grab it at the start of a job and it calls home all night. Take it on the way out, in the right bag, and get rid of the trail.

### Carrying bulky things {#sec-8-carry}

A rifle, a sledgehammer, a shotgun — anything you need **two hands** for, and anything marked **bulky** — doesn't fit in a pocket. You carry **one** bulky thing without trouble, slung on your back. A **bag** carries one more: a **Duffel Bag** (cheap, and everyone can see you're carrying something) or a **Shielded Duffel** (rare, street only: its lining blocks the signal of a tracked item inside). Only one bag counts. Pistols, SMGs, blades, magazines, cash and small loot never count.

Carry more than that and you are **Slowed** (−1 action, never below 1) for as long as you do: the sheet shows *Slowed · carrying too much* and the inventory counts *Bulky 2/1*. Put something down — in a stash, in a teammate's hands, on the floor — and it ends. Letting go is free; picking it up again in a fight is an action. Four rifles off a patrol make a good story and a bad escape.

### The washer {#sec-8-washer}

Some fixers move money between the two worlds. **Washing** turns cash into Credit Score: a fake salary, a fake contract, and your score goes up. **Cashing out** goes the other way: you borrow against your score and walk out with untraced cash, and your score goes down. The fixer names a rate (cash per Credit Score point) and takes a commission; the GM makes the offer, it appears in Gear, and you choose how many points to take — the sheet shows the sum before you agree. Credit Score never goes above its cap or below 0 this way. A fixer may also offer to **wash a hot gun**: pick which, pay their share of its price, and it comes back unlocked, untracked and yours — then a d20 decides whether the trail leads back to the crew. Every deal is logged: a fixer's books are a trail of their own.

## 8-2. Weapons {#sec-8-2}

Every weapon attacks with a skill: knives, batons and stun guns use **Melee / CQC**, pistols **Handguns**, SMGs and assault rifles **Automatic Firearms**, sniper rifles **Long Guns / Shotguns**, fists **Brawling**. Damage adds your Offensive Bonus.

::table[weapons]

### Weapon mods {#sec-8-2-mods}

Weapons take small upgrades in **mod slots** by rarity: F and E 1, D and C 2, B 3, A 4 (fists and the stun gun none). **One mod of each kind per weapon.** Melee: a *Balanced Hilt* (+1 TN) and a *Sharpened Edge* (+1 damage). Firearms: a *Custom Grip* (+1 TN), a *Heavy Barrel* (+1 damage), *Improved Sights* (+25% maximum range) an *Extended Magazine* (+50%, at least one more round) and a *Suppressor* (decision 372: the shot is **quiet** — heard only up to 2 squares instead of 8–15 — for −1 damage and −25% maximum range; not on shotguns or bows). Anyone can buy them; **a Soldier or a Tech Wizard installs and removes them**, out of a fight, for free — *🔧* on the weapon's Gear row. A modded weapon becomes its own item (marked *Modded*): its mods go with it when you sell, trade, stash or give it, and it still loads its stock magazines. So does a **unique** weapon the GM forged from a catalog gun: it shares that gun's spare magazines (decision 311). Taking off the last mod makes it a stock weapon again. Prices are provisional.

::table[weapon-mods]

## 8-3. Armor {#sec-8-3}

Each piece adds its **armor value** to your armor: your armor is the sum of what you wear (plus cover in a fight). Visibility tells how obvious the armor is to a casual observer (1 = discreet, 5 = unmistakable).

Armor **cracks** by 1 when it stops a location attack completely, or when an effect strips it. Cracks stay until the armor is **repaired** — during downtime, or with a Tech Wizard's *Field Maintenance* — or until you change what you wear. Normal hits never crack armor (see 7-5).

::table[armor]

**ID Scramble Kits** are not armor: a balaclava, gloves and a shell layer for heists and entries, worn in the full-body slot with no protection, sold for street cash only. Putting one on gives its condition, taking it off ends it. The basic kit makes you **Masked** (no visual ID, no prints or DNA — but obviously masked, so treated as hostile on sight); the enhanced kit makes you **Unreadable** (visual, thermal and gait scans fail too — but automated security flags you on sight: a monitoring alert). The GM sees the condition on your card.

## 8-4. Items & Tools {#sec-8-4}

**Using items.** Consumables — kits, stims, antidotes, grenades — have a **Use** button in the inventory, and *Use item* on the Turn card (**1 action** in combat, free outside). Pick the item and, if it offers a choice, what it does; medical items work **on you** or **on an ally next to you** (the window shows the result, e.g. "heal 7", and they enter it on their own sheet). Heals are rolled by the app or typed from your dice. **Timed effects** (+2 DEX for 3 rounds…) show as a green pill on the status bar, count down at each round end and raise every number built on them; strong stims end in a **crash** (Slowed for a round). Items sold in packs ("×5") give that many. Smoke, EMP, darts and the like change the battlefield: the sheet spends the action and logs the effect, the GM applies it.

**Tools.** Gear you carry — lockpicks, climbing hooks, AR glasses, a crowbar — helps a roll when it fits: the roll window lists the tools for that skill, attribute or attack as tick boxes ("Mechanical Lockpick Set +2 · physical locks"). Tick the one that applies; **one tool counts per roll**, nothing is used up, and the GM can say it doesn't apply. Always-on gear (AR glasses for Perception, a laser sight for gun attacks) starts ticked. The log names the tool. A **gas mask** in your inventory makes you immune to gas.

::table[items]

## 8-5. Hacker Decks & Mods {#sec-8-5}

A deck sets the Hacker's **RAM** and **heat cap**. Mod slots take hardware upgrades: **RAM expansions** add RAM, **cooling upgrades** raise the heat cap. Battery life has no rule yet — use it for narrative pressure.

::table[decks]

::table[deck-mods]

### Tech Wizard drones {#sec-8-5-drones}

The Tech Wizard's drone is an item, bought and modded like a deck — and every Tech Wizard starts with a free **Scrap Drone**, their personal project. It never takes a turn: the Tech Wizard spends actions on the abilities that use it. Its numbers go into them: the Engineer's *Deploy Sentry* barrier is the drone hovering, the sentry turret is the drone landing (its HP, armor and damage). And it gives **reach**: *Healing Surge*, *Field Maintenance* and *Rig Power Conduit*, normally next to you, work up to the drone's reach away. Mod slots take a longer link (reach), a frame (HP), plating (armor), a payload (turret damage) or a shield emitter (armor for allies behind the barrier). When it's shot down, mark it **Wrecked** on the engine: it never shuts down, but it works at half reach and half damage until *Restock & Repair* or the time between missions fixes it. All values are provisional.

::table[drones]

::table[drone-mods]

### Scavenger optics {#sec-8-5-optics}

An optic sets the Scavenger's **Range**, **Track** and **mode** (5-6). Anyone can buy one; only a Scavenger uses the numbers.

::table[optics]

## Ammo, misc items and trading {#sec-8-ammo}

**Magazines.** Every gun has its own spare magazine in the market's *Ammo* tab (your guns' magazines come first, marked *fits your gun*). A magazine costs 10% of the gun's price (at least $5), and **every gun comes with 2 spares**. *Reload* loads a spare and discards the one in the gun; with no spares left you can't reload — buy some, or ask the GM. The weapon card shows how many spares you have.

**Bursts.** SMGs and assault rifles fire **3-round bursts**: each attack uses 3 rounds, so a 30-round magazine is 10 attacks. The sheet counts it for you ("10 attacks left"). Abilities use the ammunition they say.

**Custom items.** The market's *Misc* tab adds anything worth tracking — a keycard, a sealed package, a photo — to a *Misc* group: no price, no rules. If it cost money, change your cash by hand.

**Unique items.** Sometimes you find something one of a kind — an experimental smart pistol, a heavily modded deck. The GM forges it and sends it to whoever found it; it shows in **purple** with a *unique* tag, with a one-line note ("◆ Smart-link: locks onto body heat") on the weapon card. Its bonuses apply while it's in use: a weapon in a slot, armor worn, the active deck or optic, an item carried. It keeps every stat if you hand it to someone, it's never on sale, and some can't be fenced at all. Its magazines are in the market's Ammo tab like any other gun's.

**Trash and trade.** Every inventory row has 🗑 (drop one, after asking) and ⇄ (trade): **sell** it for half the list price — a quarter if you bought it officially (registered gear is built to age fast; street-bought units are sold first) — (custom items: type what you got), or **give** it to a player at the table — it lands on their sheet even if their tablet is asleep, and in combat it costs 1 action (Interact). Everything is logged, so the GM sees where things went.

## 8-6. Lifestyle {#sec-8-6}

The monthly cost of existing. Operators start at the bottom; moving up is something you earn. Pick yours in the market's Lifestyle tab with **Choose**: it charges nothing. Some categories hold **one** choice and a new one replaces it — housing, data plan, food: a new flat replaces the old one. Others **add up** — bills, transport, fees, a club — except where two entries are alternatives of each other (basic or premium medical, plain or ad-free streams). Each market row says which (*adds to the others* / *replaces the one you have*); ✕ removes one. The sheet shows the monthly upkeep in $ (or ≈ Credit Score if paid officially); after a downtime you pay it for the time that passed — cash, Credit Score or half and half (5-10). The cheapest plans have a catch: the **ad-supported data plan** clutters your lens (−1 *Perception / Awareness*); **company bodega rations** wear you down (1 less Strain recovered in downtime); a **capsule bunk** is the home that recovers least.

::table[lifestyle]
